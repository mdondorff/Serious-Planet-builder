# G-LOCAL on the f32 rounds (P1 item E2), R3a and R1

Run 2026-11-12 by the spikes lane on the lead's order (inbox #104). Reported with numbers; the verdict is the lead's.
Main f4e135e9 (GEN-166 f32 rounds archived, #1447; GEN-174 round-0 breach and its per-round cap in `catchment_round`; `exit_window_cut`, GEN-172). Release build, Windows, 8 threads, BelowNormal, margin 200 (1,020 x 1,020 = 1,040,400 cells a window): R3a (seed 15, face 4) and R1 (seed 14, face 1). Rounds 1 (from the base) and 3 (after two carved rounds), `round_f32` on (`CarveParams::round_f32`, as the default `BuildParams` set it).
Probe: `gate_probe local` and `gate_probe keys` on the local branch `spike/glocal-f32` (the SPIKE-106 probe of `spike/gate-remeasure` ported to this main; spike code, never merged). Raw output: `glocal-f32/`.

## What changed in the probe
- The own kernel (`my_round`, written from the rules, not from `catchment_round`) narrows its output to f32 at the end of the round (GEN-166: the round computes in f64 and the surface is stored f32); the stitched surface is narrowed for every cell, single-cell catchments included, as the library narrows the whole surface.
- The own kernel takes the breach cap per cell per round of GEN-174 (a cell is lowered by at most `breach_max_m` from its height at the start of the round, over all events of the round). That rule was read from `round.rs` and the GEN-174 commit message, so the agreement on it (L1b below) is not independent.
- `CatchmentInputs::exit_window_cut` is false in every run (`CarveParams::window_cut` is off by default): GEN-172's window-cut exit is not exercised.
- Control: the same build with `PLANET_ROUND_F32=0` (f64 rounds on today's main) separates what f32 changes from what other changes on main since 2026-11-10 changed.

## L1 trace (inbox, lead, 2026-11-12: R3a round 1, the answer is "probe stale" or "library departs from the spec")

**Answer: the probe is stale.** The library follows GEN-173; the probe's flood does not. Evidence, R3a round 1 (and round 3, R1 in the raw files):
1. Receivers: 35,570 of 1,040,400 cells (round 3: 4,942) have another receiver than the library's. The probe's receiver rule (steepest jittered drop on the filled surface, A3 line 422 of `terrain-target.md`) applied to the **library's** filled surface equals the library's receivers in 0 of 1,040,400 cells (L1d), so the receiver rule is not the difference. The cells where it differs sit in flats of the filled surface (examples in the raw output: two neighbours at the same filled level, the other level one epsilon lower or equal).
2. The filled surface: the probe's flood against the library's graded flood (`route_rules_filled`): 110,050 cells differ on R3a round 1 (library lower in 108,268, higher in 1,782; max 0.038 m; round 3: 21,602 cells, max 0.126 m). The probe's flood equals the library's **ungraded** canonical flood (`priority_flood_ungraded`) bit for bit (L1e). The library's graded surface is GEN-173 (flats graded by distance to the pour points, #1385, merged 2026-11-12, after the last measurement of this gate on 2026-11-10).
3. The member order is not the cause (the first reading was wrong): where the own and the library's member lists are the same set, the own kernel on the probe's inputs in the probe's order and in the library's order differ from the library by the same amount (R3a round 1: 207,157 and 211,049 cells, max 70.4 m, all from the other flood); with the library's graded flood both are 0 (L1c). Both orders are topological (0 positions with the receiver after the cell in either list).
4. With the library's graded surface substituted for the probe's flood (`PLANET_FLOOD_LIB=1`; everything else stays the probe's own: receivers, catchments, `A_d`, distances, taper, kernel with the cap and f32 narrowing): **L1 is 0 of 1,040,400 cells in 4 of 4 runs** (R3a and R1, rounds 1 and 3), the member sets are equal in every catchment (6,387 of 6,387, 6,996 of 6,996, 2,541, 2,508), receivers differ in 0 cells, L1c 0, and L2 and L4 stay 0.
5. What stays lost: the independence of the flood itself. The probe does not re-implement GEN-173's grade (the spec text leaves open how the flat and its rim are found and how a cell is "kept above the neighbour it drains to", so an own implementation would not reproduce the bits from the text). The grade is checked by G-PART's tile equality and by `bake_flats`, not here.
6. A sentence of GEN-173 is broken by a small amount: "No cell SHALL stand above its chessboard count". Against the library's ungraded flood, the graded surface is lower in 108,268 cells and higher in 1,782 (R3a round 1; round 3 334; R1 round 1 3,300 and round 3 213), by at most 9.1e-13 m in round 1 and 9.8e-10 m in round 3. The excess is far above one ulp of f64 at these heights, so it is not plain rounding; its cause is not derived (a bound with a formula, or a fix, is open and parked by the lead).

## Limits
- Two windows, two rounds, one flip of one rule (clip) in the memoised solve; `canonical_flood` refusal and the `Schedule` field not tested; `breach_height_scale_m` above 0 is in the key test only (the own kernel implements the scale-free breach).
- `exit_window_cut` false everywhere: the window-cut exit of GEN-172, which the K7 and region builds use, is not covered.
- The own kernel's breach cap is read from the library (not independent).
- L1 with the library's graded flood (the probe's flood predates GEN-173 and is not re-implemented): the flood's independence is lost in this probe.
- Reported, not gated.
