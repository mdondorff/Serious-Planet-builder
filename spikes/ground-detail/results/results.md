# SPIKE-27 results: ground detail at the lowest levels (G1, with the near-plane check G0) (ground-detail)

M7 step G1 of `docs/archive/M7/m7-planning.md`, with the owner's addendum (a crouched eye at about 0.5 m, a near plane of a few centimetres). Prototype code: branch `spike/ground-detail` (never merged; `ground-detail-prototype.patch` is its diff against main at 257ebe86). Machine: Rasierklinge, RTX 3080 Ti Laptop (Vulkan), release build, other sessions running (the timings are indicative only; no perf window).

## Answer in one paragraph
**The smear is the shading, not the geometry: the finest tiles have 7.5 cm vertices, but nothing varies on them.** The albedo is a function of the tile's smooth climate, the slope and the height; the relief of the field stops at 12 m cells (SPIKE-07); the light is Lambert with no roughness. A procedural, world-anchored detail term per material (rock, sand, grass chosen by the albedo's own slope and precipitation weights), four noise octaves from 2 m down to 3 cm, turns the flat fields into recognisable ground at 1.8 m and 0.5 m within about 15 m, for **+0.45 to +0.85 ms of GPU time at 2560 x 1440** and a node record that grows by 128 bytes (a quick prototype's size, reducible). It needs no assets. The near plane is not a problem: reversed-Z `Depth32Float` has the same relative depth resolution for any near value, and 10, 5 and 2 cm look identical over the finest tiles, down to an eye 12 cm above the ground. What stands in the way of a crouch is the viewer's 1 m ground clamp (VIEW-004), not the renderer. Recommendation: **a hybrid, procedural first**: the prototype's per-material detail as the always-on, deterministic, asset-free baseline; CC0 albedo and normal sets as an optional close-range layer from a fetched cache, not from the repository.

## (2) The options
All are visual only: heights, collision and picking stay with `height(dir)` (ADR 0028). The cost figures are the prototype's, measured; the others are estimates, marked.
| Option | Look | Cost at 1440p (RTX) | Memory | CON-11 and determinism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Procedural per-material detail (the prototype: value noise octaves at 2 m, 0.5 m, 12.5 cm, 3 cm; albedo multiplier and normal tilt by material) | rock mottled with cracks, sand grain and ripples, grass blade-scale patches; stylised, no photographic detail; the amplitudes need tuning (below) | **+0.45 to +0.85 ms** measured (8 noise lookups per octave pixel, 4 octaves, fading with the pixel footprint) | none for assets; 128 bytes per node record (208 to 336, +62 %) in the prototype | the lattices are anchored to the world by integer cells and fractions from f64 on the CPU (like the relief grid's decade phases), so no planet-radius value reaches a shader; integer hash, identical on every adapter; the pixels are not a generator output (CON-14 does not apply) |
| B. Detail textures (albedo, normal, roughness per material, CC0 sets), planar or triplanar, stochastic tiling | photographic close up (pebbles, blades, soil); repetition has to be hidden (stochastic or hex tiling, 2 to 3 extra lookups) | estimate: 3 maps x 3 lookups (stochastic) x materials blended by weight, about 0.3 to 0.8 ms; not measured | 1K set (albedo BC7, normal BC5, roughness BC4) about 2.5 MB with mips; 6 materials 15 MB; at 2K 60 MB (estimates) | UVs = fraction + local / scale from the same anchored phases (decades 1 cm to 1,000 km exist in the node record), so no new data for power-of-ten scales; sampling is adapter-exact enough for a look |
| C. Detail normals only (SPIKE-07's term) | relief without colour change: it reads as bumpy, not as rock versus sand | about 0.3 to 0.5 ms (one gradient sum) | none | as A |
| D. Hybrid: A everywhere, B only within about 10 m for the nearest materials | the procedural look at distance and in tests, the photographic one at the feet | A plus B's cost on a few pixels | as A plus the sets | as A and B |
Material choice per pixel (all options): slope (the albedo's `cs`), precipitation (grass against sand), temperature, height above the sea (beach), and later the region's surface type (G2), through the same weights as `albedo_of`, so the colour and the detail never disagree.

## The prototype (what was built)
- `crates/render/src/terrain.rs`: the node record gains four lattices (integer cell of the tile origin from f64, fraction, cells per metre; scales 2 m, 0.5 m, 12.5 cm, 3 cm) and the WGSL `ground_detail`: 3D value noise with analytic gradients, per-material weights from the albedo table's own thresholds, rock = cracked ridged mottling, sand = fine grain and low ripples, grass = blade-scale patches with strong small normals; octaves fade while a pixel covers under 0.4 of a cell; the albedo multiplier and the tilted normal feed `shade_lit` and `atm_shade_lit`. `crates/app/src/litperf.rs`: `CAPTURE_GROUND=1 test-render --scene capture-flights ...` writes stills at chosen eye heights and times the first one (100 frames, GPU timestamps). `GROUND_DETAIL=1` switches the term on, `GROUND_NEAR_M`, `GROUND_POSES`, `GROUND_SITES`, `GROUND_MATERIAL` steer the captures. A first version had the normal gradient without the cell size and tilted the normals to near 90 degrees (`GROUND_DETAIL` results shown are after the fix).
- A `GROUND_CRATER` hook meant to put a steep slope in the world did not take effect (the stamp did not change the height at the site); it is in the patch and unused. The 256-cell bake has no slope over 25 m in 300 m, so no site had the rock material naturally; the rock look is shown with the material forced (`images/cmp_materials.jpg`).

## Evidence
**Before and after at 1.8 m** (`images/cmp_eye1.8.jpg`, rows: grass, grass, sand, desert; left plain, right with the term): the grass becomes tufts, the sand granular, the desert rippled and shadowed; the horizon region stays flat.
**Crouched, 0.5 m** (`images/cmp_eye0.5.jpg`): the same, the texture reading as ground within about 15 m.
**At 20 m** (`images/cmp_eye20.jpg`): the finer octaves have faded (grass looks as before); the desert's 2 m and 0.5 m octaves stay resolved and look too regular and too contrasty (dark blotches where the low sun grazes a tilted normal): the amplitude of the sand ripples is too high, an artefact of the first tuning.
**Materials side by side** (`images/cmp_materials.jpg`, the same ground with the material forced to rock, sand, grass): distinguishable by structure alone (cracked and mottled, fine grain, blades); the colour is still the climate's green there, so a rock needs the albedo's own grey, which it gets on real steep ground.
**Timing** (`CAPTURE_GROUND`, 2560 x 1440, lit with the atmosphere, 100 frames each, GPU timestamps; three repeats each, the repeats disturbed by other sessions are left out): eye 1.8 m, pitch 6: plain 1.795 and 1.795 ms, with the term 2.638 and 2.662 ms (+0.85 ms); eye 0.5 m, pitch 25: plain 1.775 and 1.736 ms, with the term 2.267 and 2.179 ms (+0.45 ms). The frame is nearly empty (flat ground), so the term is most of the difference; a loaded view adds nothing but the same per-pixel work.

## G0: the near plane and the depth
- **Current value:** 0.1 m. **Depth precision:** reversed-Z with a 32-bit float depth and an infinite far plane stores `near / d`; the relative resolution of a float is 2^-23 at worst, so the depth step is about `d x 1.2e-7` whatever the near value: about 12 micrometres at 100 m, 1.2 mm at 10 km and 1.4e-8 m at 0.12 m. A smaller near plane costs no depth precision (a standard property of reversed-Z with a float buffer, here computed, not measured from the buffer).
- **Pictures** (`images/cmp_near.jpg`, `images/cmp_stress.jpg`): the same poses with near 10 cm, 5 cm and 2 cm are identical to the eye at eye 0.5 m (grass and desert) and in the stress poses (eye 12 cm above the ground looking 35 degrees down, eye 50 cm looking 60 degrees down): **no z-fighting, no clipping into the ground, no flicker of the tiles' seams**; the ground nearest to the eye is farther than the near plane in every pose (at 12 cm, looking down 35 degrees, the nearest ground along the view is about 21 cm). Clipping starts only when the eye is within the near distance of the surface, which a 5 cm near plane at an eye of 0.5 m or more cannot reach.
- **Not tested:** the aerial-perspective volume and the water pass with a near plane of 5 cm (the water pass and the AP volume start at the camera); moving the camera at walking speed over uneven ground; the mesh against the field height at the feet (the mesh is the field's lattice at 7.5 cm, so the difference is below a centimetre on smooth relief).
- **What blocks a crouch today:** the viewer's 1 m ground clamp (VIEW-004); the near plane value is set where the camera is built (`viewer.rs`, `scenes.rs`, `freecam.rs` pass it through).

## (3) The asset question
- **Procedural only (option A)** is complete and deterministic, costs no repository space and no network and is what tests and CI should use; its limit is the look: stylised, no photographic soil or pebbles; the amplitudes and the octave mix are tuning work (a day or two per material with the owner's eye).
- **CC0 sets (option B):** ambientCG (CC0 1.0) and Poly Haven (CC0) publish PBR material sets; the licences need no attribution but the sources are recorded (ADR 0027's licence rule, `cargo deny` does not cover assets): a table in the ADR listing set name, URL, licence, resolution and SHA-256. Typical size of a 1K set as PNG or JPEG is 1 to 6 MB (estimate, not downloaded here: a download needs the owner's go, and none was made), so one set already crosses the 1 MB rule, six cross it many times.
- **Handling:** keep the repository free of assets: `cargo xtask assets fetch` downloads pinned URLs with checksums into a per-user cache (outside `target/`, so a clean does not lose it), the renderer reads the cache when it is present and falls back to option A when it is not; goldens and CI use option A so they stay deterministic and offline; a bake never depends on assets (they are presentation, like the look file). The ADR that decides this (assets outside the repository, the 1 MB rule for assets) is the one the owner reads before G2.
- **Owner's Q9 answer (both, procedural variation):** fits the hybrid: the procedural term supplies the variation and the fallback, the sets supply the close-range photographic look.

## Recommendation (for G2)
1. Build option D in this order: the procedural per-material term (A) first, production-grade: tune the amplitudes (the sand ripples are too strong), add two mid-scale octaves (8 m, 32 m) so the ground is not smooth beyond 15 m (the grass at 20 m is as flat as before), pass the region's surface type as the material selector (WDEF-012), and cut the node record cost (one integer cell per node plus the existing decade phases for the fractions, about +16 bytes instead of +128).
2. Then the optional CC0 layer (B) behind the asset cache, stochastic tiling, within 10 m.
3. Budget proposal after this evidence: ground detail at most 1.0 ms of GPU at 1440p on the RTX in a ground view (the prototype uses 0.45 to 0.85 ms with four octaves); a tier-C test with a 1.8 m and a 0.5 m still per material (goldens through `golden-review`) and a tier-A test that the lattices are world-anchored (two neighbouring tiles agree at their shared edge).
4. Near plane 5 cm with the eye clamp lowered to 0.5 m (VIEW-004); test the AP volume and the water pass at the new near plane first (the open item above).
5. Not decided here: roughness and specular (the renderer has no specular term at all; the "roughness" map of the owner's list needs one), vegetation instancing (I1).
