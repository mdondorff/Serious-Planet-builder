# SPIKE-111: building the coarse tree lattice across the cube-face edges (design note, nothing built)

Written 2026-11-12 by the spikes lane for the architect's second item (the lattice as one graph over the six faces' coarse cells). A description from reading the code; no probe was run for it; the numbers are estimates from the probe's measured rates (`results.md`). No verdict.

## What is stored
- A world-level lattice of `cf = 4` coarse cells (3.3 km at the 12,288-cell faces of the regional solve): n = 12,288 / 4 = 3,072 cells a face edge, 9.4 million cells a face, 56.6 million for the six faces.
- Per cell the distance in metres to the nearest channel cell of each area tier (16, 64, 256 km2), quantised to u16 (a log scale or 1 m steps up to the hillslope length, saturated at that length: the relief uses `min(d / L, 1)`): 3 x 2 B = 340 MB for the world; one value a cell (the relief-weighted distance sum, if the tiers are folded at build time) is 113 MB. The tiers' weights and lengths are rule parameters, so a one-value lattice fixes them at build time (a change of the rule rebuilds; a rule key in the lattice header).
- A pure function of (seed, e0 parameters, the rule): CON-30 holds once stored (the same lattice read at a point whatever the tile partition).

## The build as one graph (the existing cube grid)
- `bake/cube.rs` (GEN-144) already joins the six faces as one grid of `6 n^2` cells ("a cell has its 8 neighbours inside its face and, across an edge, the one axis neighbour of the face it meets; a diagonal across an edge or a corner is not an edge of the graph", areas and distances the sphere's); `Cube::new(n, radius)`, `neighbour(p, d)`, `area(p)`, `distance(p, q)` exist and H0 routes on it. The lattice build uses it at n = 3,072 with the probe's steps, in the graph's own metric:
  1. e0 of every coarse cell: the block mean of the band-free surface (`BakeInputs::base` with the band off) over the 4 x 4 cells of the 12,288 grid inside it (16 e0 evaluations a cell: 906 million evaluations for the world, about 15 us each = 3.8 core-hours, 28 minutes on 8 threads; or a point sample at the coarse cell centre, 57 million evaluations, 3 minutes on one thread, at the cost of aliasing in the rough belts: not measured). e0 is smooth, so the point sample is the first thing to try.
  2. Route the coarse surface on the cube graph (D8, the canonical flood with the epsilon as the regional rounds, the sea cells as outlets): one routing over 56.6 million cells; the probe's 1.1 us a cell gives about 62 s on one thread. Upstream area in m2 from the sphere's cell areas; a basin of e0 is filled and routed over its spill (the tree crosses it along the filled surface, as the window form does).
  3. Channel cells per tier: area at least 16, 64, 256 km2 and not sea.
  4. Distance to the nearest channel cell per tier: a multi-source Dijkstra over the same graph with the great-circle edge lengths (the probe's chamfer is the planar special case; across a face edge the graph gives the sphere's distance with the usual cube-edge distortion of the 8-neighbour metric, as the routing already has). A bucketed or binary-heap Dijkstra is about 0.2 to 0.5 us a cell: 10 to 30 s a tier.
  5. Quantise and write the lattice per face; keep a one-cell halo copied from the neighbour face (below).
- Memory of the build: z f32, receiver u32, order u32, area f32 for 56.6 million cells is about 0.9 GB, the flood's queue and the distance arrays about 1 GB more: about 2 GB, well inside the 12 GB build budget of the world build; a face-at-a-time build is not needed (the whole graph is routed at once, as H0's global grid is).
- Time: about 3 to 5 minutes on one thread plus step 1 (3 minutes point-sampled, 28 minutes block-averaged on 8 threads); the world build is hours, so the lattice is a small stage; run once per world after e0's parameters are fixed (the band does not enter: e0 is band-free).

## Reading it at a point
- `direction_to_face(d)` gives the face and the plane coordinates; the lattice is read like the class lattice and the global lattice are: bilinear over the four coarse cells around the point, or the C1 read of the global store when a smoother field is wanted (a distance field with a kink at the channels needs no more than bilinear).
- Across a face edge the four cells of one face's read would leave the face: store a one-cell halo per face (the 3,072-cell face becomes 3,074 a side) filled from the neighbour face's cells through `Cube::resolve`; both faces then read the same cells at a point of the edge (the cell centres of the two faces do not coincide on the cube's warped plane, so the halo cell is the neighbour face's cell at its own centre; the read error at the edge is the interpolation error of one coarse cell, below the 3.3 km scale of the field). At a cube corner (three faces) the corner halo cell is the average of the two neighbours (the diagonal across a corner is not a graph edge). The class lattice and the global lattice use nodes on the face edge with identical values from both faces; a node-centred variant of this lattice would share those node values by construction, at the cost of building the graph on shared edge nodes.
- Cost per sample: a lattice read of 4 values (about 10 ns) plus the tier weights and the smoothstep: against about 5 to 6 us of one base evaluation, the relief of the tree is cheaper than the plain band's noise octaves.

## Risks and open points (not measured)
- The lattice's resolution against the tree's fineness: at cf 4 the fine channel structure below 3.3 km is lost (R1h relief p50 / p95 62 / 368 m against 92 / 498 m for the window form at the same gain); the gain is the lever (see the gain note when run).
- The jittered receiver or a grade on flats (the straight-edge artefact) is a routing choice of step 2 and applies on the coarse graph the same way.
- Seeds and worlds with other radii: the lattice scales with n; the area thresholds are in km2 and scale with the body (the same as `RIDGED_WAVELENGTHS_M`, `scale = radius / EARTH_RADIUS_M`).
- An edit of e0 (an uplift edit) changes the tree: the lattice would be rebuilt for the edited region (a local re-route is not exact: the routing is global); the editing design (the explorer's edit refresh) needs a decision whether the start surface's tree follows edits or is frozen at world creation.
