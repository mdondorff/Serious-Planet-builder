## Machine-state findings (input for ADR 0094)
1. **Heat soak.** After more than 1 h of near-continuous load (bake, step 2, 3b, crusader, round 1), the single kernel read a steady 5-7 % slow (54-56 k us) despite the 16-45 s duty idle; at 07:50-08:05 the same machine read 46.9-50.0 k. A duty idle between runs does not undo a heat soak; a long cool-down (or a temperature reading) is needed before a window that follows sustained load.
2. **Vendor performance profile.** The owner changed the Razer Synapse performance settings about 10:10. After a 12 min idle the parallel median read 127,830-135,528 us (morning 67,023-94,992: about +75 %), singles 46,267-55,473 (noisier, not faster); the Windows power plan was unchanged (Höchstleistung) and WMI shows only the 2,400 MHz base clock. The vendor profile is a machine state the gate cannot see: a window's record names it, and windows under different profiles are never mixed in one verdict.
   - After the owner restored the pre-10:10 CPU and GPU settings (fan kept at maximum), 2 preflights at 10:31-10:34 still read the parallel median at 128,042-132,958 us (singles 46,170-56,083): reverting the profile did not restore the all-core speed.
   - All-core limited after the Synapse change; cause open (a package power limit or an EC state). It is not the 2026-10-29 latch, which read about 7x slow on single and parallel (373-410 ms single, 17 % processor performance); today singles are normal and only parallel is about +40-75 % (opus-planner, 2026-11-03).
   - Processor performance at 10:35 (WMI `PercentProcessorPerformance`, `_Total`, nominal 2,160 MHz): idle 143-155 %; under 18 busy loops for 30 s 105-121 %, settling at 107-109 %, with processor utility only 43-49 % (the loops did not saturate every thread). No morning reference exists. The owner decides on an EC reset or the Synapse profile.
3. **Strays.** One orphaned single-core process (the `find`) put every single reading over its limit while the parallel ones passed; the preflight's top-process list named it (4.9 %, max 5.0 %).
