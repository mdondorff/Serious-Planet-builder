# SPIKE-29 results: edit scale (edit-scale)

Opened: 2026-10-22
Question: How far do authored edits scale (100, 1 k, 5 k, 20 k items, 80 % in one 50 km area, a realistic mix of stamps, lines, regions and brushes), what breaks first, and is the plan job the bottleneck?
Why it matters: the owner asked (2026-10-22); ADR 0066's revisit trigger is 20 k items; the plan job over authored items is already tracked (windows 23 to 25: seconds cold, 0.4 to 0.7 s warm).
Time box: about 3 h of agent work (opus-planner, 2026-10-22); indicative on the RTX, no perf window.
Success criteria: for each N, the item load time and memory, the plan job p50 and p95 cold and warm at 2 km, 200 m and 30 m over the cluster and over empty ground, the generation time of a tile in the cluster, the cost of the field's height and bounds queries, the editor's per-frame CPU with the editor on, and the edit-to-visible latency of one hill added in the cluster; the first thing that breaks, with its cause in the code; fixes ranked by gain.
Fallback if it fails: report what ran and where it stopped.
Code location: the generator and the measurements are kept in the repo as the scene `test-render --scene edit-scale` (`crates/app/src/editscale.rs`), reusable; the results and raw logs are in spikes/edit-scale/results/.

## Answer in one paragraph
**The first thing that breaks is not the plan job but the editor's own lists, at about 100 items; the plan job is the next and the worst, and it grows with the item count even over empty ground.** After every edit the editor recomputes the steep and stacked brush lists, which cost 0.3 s at 100 items (the stacked list is the Problems tab of A5) and 18 s at 1,000 (quadratic in the strokes). The plan job over authored items costs 20 ms at 100 items, 3.5 to 4.9 s warm (7 to 14 s cold) at 1,000, 18 to 20 s warm at 5,000 and 67 to 92 s warm at 20,000, over the cluster and over empty ground alike: from 1,000 items on, the proof that no tile is too steep (LOD-012, `none_steep`) fails, and every plan then runs the whole position-only walk and the ceiling pass (2.3 + 1.7 s of the 4.4 s at 1,000 items) without a cache between plans, a cost that follows the number of items within the coarse levels' reach (the 20 % spread over the planet count). The editor's frame costs 5 ms at 100 items, 50 to 70 ms at 1,000, 5.4 s at 5,000 and 94 s at 20,000 (it outlines every edit under a wanted coarse tile, 15 k of 20 k, and each outline samples a field whose cost in the cluster follows the items: 24 us at 100, 2.2 ms at 20,000). What does not break: an edit itself (31 to 35 ms a put at every size, 60 ms with brush strokes; the definition clone is 0.2 ms at 1,000 and 4 ms at 20,000), the generation of a tile (2.7 ms at 100, 6.6 ms at 20,000 in the cluster), the swap of the definition, and everything over ground with no item in reach. Ranked fixes: cache the ceiling across plans or make the proof local, bound the editor's shown set and outlines, make the Problems lists linear (A5's own cost), a finer item index inside a cell (and the cached stamp bounds of the Ridge 3 entry), a lazy and parallel load.

## Method
The console lines of the runs whose full output was not kept (the first-frame lists, the stage timer, the 1,000-item set without brushes, the edit-latency runs) are in `run-extra-console-lines.txt`, copied as printed. During a warm plan the counting field saw 0 calls except 4 `height_bounds` and 4 `inputs_hash_for` (1.3 ms in all) in the 1,001-item cluster run. The plan figures per N are medians of 12 warm plans at 100 and 1,001 items and one warm plan at 5,000 and 20,000 (the diagnosis run at 1,001 items used 3); the per-item cost of the warm plan over empty ground is 4.4, 3.9 and 4.6 ms an item at 1,000, 5,000 and 20,000 items.
- The scene `test-render --scene edit-scale --store DIR` (kept in the repo: `crates/app/src/editscale.rs`, CPU only) and its environment (`PLANET_SCALE_COUNTS`, `_DIR`, `_SEED`, `_FRAMES`, `_ALTS`, `_NOBRUSH`, `_NOPLAN`). RTX machine, release build, one process at a time, other sessions running: indicative (no gate, no perf window). The world is the editor's default (eroded field) over the 16384-cell store `target/accept/global16384`.
- The item sets: made through the editor session, as a user would (`Session::place`, the line and region tools, the brush tool with up to 64 strokes in an item), seeded and deterministic, each stage copied from one growing session so the set of 1,000 contains the set of 100. 80 % of the actions are in a 25 km disc round the place the `edit-latency` scene edits, the rest anywhere on the planet. The mix by action: hills 22 %, craters 18 %, plateaus 12 %, ridges 8 %, channels 5 %, regions 5 %, brush piles 30 % (1 to 64 strokes near one place, kinds raise, lower, smooth and flatten). The sets: 100 items (56 brushes of 283 strokes, 2 channels, 12 craters, 13 hills, 7 plateaus, 2 regions, 8 ridges) and 1,001 (513 brushes of 4,142 strokes, at most 64 in an item, 38 channels, 119 craters, 149 hills, 83 plateaus, 33 regions, 66 ridges). **The sets of 5,000 and 20,000 were made without brush items** (the same mix over the other kinds: 593 channels, 930 craters, 1,086 hills, 818 plateaus, 595 regions, 978 ridges at 5,000; 4 times as many at 20,000): a brush pile costs 60 ms to make, and the sets would have taken hours. The brush-specific costs (the lists) are therefore measured at 100 and 1,000 only.
- Measured per set: the load of the files into an editor (`EditRuntime::with_ids`), the working set before and after, the clone and swap of the definition, the field's `height`, `height_bounds` and `inputs_hash_for` per call over the cluster and over empty ground (1,500 and 300 samples), the plan job (`plan_terrain_cached`, 2560x1440, cells 32, tau 6 px) cold (a fresh field and selection cache) and warm (the camera drifting 0.5 m a frame; 12 plans, 3 at 1,000, 1 at 5,000 and 20,000) at 2 km, 200 m and 30 m over the cluster centre and over empty ground (40 S 100 E; the 20 % spread items are the nearest), the generation of the tiles of the finest levels in the cluster, and the editor's `update` (relevance, outlines, lists, the Problems tab) with the mode on.
- The edit-to-visible latency of one stamp in the cluster is the `edit-latency` scene over the same files (`PLANET_EDIT_ITEMS=DIR`), as a smoke run (768x432, 200 m, a 50 m stamp, a 150 m brush, a 2 km region, no gate).

## What breaks first, and why (in the code)
1. **The editor's lists after every change (from about 100 items; at 1,000 items 18 s).** `Session::steep_items` (VIEW-043, `slope::steep_items`) and `Session::stacked_items` (A5, `problems::stack_depths`) are cached per revision, but each recompute tests every stroke point against every stroke with the great-circle `Poly::distance` (O(points x strokes)); a brush edit or an undo invalidates them, and the next frame pays: 50 + 249 ms at 100 items, 3.6 + 14.4 s at 1,000 (a first attempt with a 2 km grid index gave only 1.6 times: the distance function, not the candidate count, dominates). `can_merge` of the Problems rows (`problems::merge_stack`, pairwise overlap per row) adds 1.5 s at 1,000. The stacked list and `can_merge` are A5's own cost (the verifier noted the per-frame risk and the cache fixed the repeat, not the recompute).
2. **The plan job, from about 1,000 items (3.5 to 14 s; 20,000 items: 67 to 150 s).** `select_nodes_in_view_cached` (`lod.rs`, `select_impl`) takes the fast path only when `none_steep` proves that no tile within the camera's reach is too steep to morph (LOD-012); with 1,000 items that proof fails everywhere, because some tile of the coarse levels within reach holds an item whose bounds are too wide (or the proof's visit cap, 4,000, is spent), also over ground thousands of kilometres from the cluster (the 200 items spread over the planet are within reach of the coarse levels). It then runs `walk_leaves` without the frustum over the whole position-only selection and `Ceiling::of_unmorphable` over its leaves, every plan, with nothing remembered between plans: 4 s of 4.4 s at 1,000 items; the cost follows the items within the coarse levels' reach (4.4 s at 1,000, 19.6 s at 5,000, 91.9 s at 20,000 over empty ground: about 4 to 4.6 ms an item). The window's plan worker runs the same code for every plan: the Ridge 3 entry (the plan job over authored items: seconds cold, 0.4 to 0.7 s warm over 77 items) is probably this path at a smaller N (not isolated).
3. **The editor's frame, from about 1,000 items (50 to 70 ms; 5.4 s at 5,000; 94 s at 20,000).** `Session::shown` (the footprint find over the wanted tiles) returns every edit whose footprint overlaps a wanted tile, and the wanted tiles include the coarse tiles above the finest ones, which cover the whole cluster: 3,869 of 5,000 and 15,451 of 20,000 edits are "shown" at 200 m while the camera sees a few dozen; `overlay::build` outlines each of them, sampling the field along the outline, and the field costs 24 us (100 items) to 2.2 ms (20,000) a call in the cluster. The lists (`near`, `layer_groups`, `chain`) are cheap by comparison: 0.4, 0.5 and 0.2 ms at 1,000 items, 1.9, 9 and 1.5 ms at 5,000, 8, 419 and 6.5 ms at 20,000.
4. **The field in the cluster (per query, linear in the items of the cell).** `height` 24 us at 100 items, 138 us at 1,000, 517 us at 5,000, 2.2 ms at 20,000; the bounds and `inputs_hash_for` the same. Over empty ground nothing changes (6 to 17 us at every N): the cell index (one cell is `CELL_EDGE_M`, 40 km, so a 50 km area with 16,000 items is a few cells) works between cells and not inside one, and the stamp stage evaluates every item of the cells its cap touches. This is the base of 2 and 3 and of the cold plan (7 to 14 s at 1,000 items).
5. **The load (4.2 ms an item: 86 s at 20,000 items)**, linear: a file read, parse, validation and a put into the index per item, serially, at the start of the editor.
6. **Not a problem up to 20,000:** the put (31 to 35 ms without brushes, 60 to 63 with, flat), the clone and swap of the definition (4 ms at 20,000, the ADR 0066 revisit trigger is not reached by this cost), the generation of a tile (6.6 ms at 20,000), memory (2 to 12 KB an item: 20,000 items without brushes are 36 MB, 20,000 with the 1,001-item mix would be about 200 MB).

## Recommendation (ranked by gain)
1. **Plan job: remember the ceiling.** The position-only leaves and the ceiling depend on the camera only through the coarse levels' cells: keep them in the `SelectionCache` keyed by the camera's tile at a coarse level (and the item revision) and extend them lazily as the camera moves, or make the proof local (certify subtrees one by one and run the full path only under the uncertified ones, which are the tiles near an item). At 1,000 items 4 s of 4.4 s go; over ground far from items the plan returns to the fast path whatever N is. Expected gain: 100 to 1,000 times at 20,000 items (92 s to well under a second over empty ground; over the cluster the ceiling near the items remains).
2. **Editor frame: bound what is shown.** Take the edits whose reach covers at least a few pixels and are within the view, nearest first, at most a few hundred, and outline only those; sample the outline's heights once per edit and revision. At 20,000 items from 94 s to the cost of a few hundred outlines.
3. **A5 and VIEW-043 lists: linear and lazy.** A chord distance (planar) instead of the great-circle one, a grid over the strokes, the stacked list and `can_merge` computed only when the Problems tab is shown and not after every edit (or incrementally from the strokes an edit changed). 18 s to milliseconds at 1,000 items. This is A5's regression and should be fixed first by size of the freeze (0.3 s a change at 100 items already).
4. **A finer item index inside a cell and the cached stamp bounds per item footprint** (the Ridge 3 entry): a 2 km hash grid under the 40 km cells makes `height` and `height_bounds` in a dense cluster 10 to 100 times cheaper; caching the stamp stage's bounds per item footprint removes most of the cold plan's field queries (7 to 14 s at 1,000 items).
5. **Load lazily and in parallel:** read the item files per cell as tiles need them, or in parallel (4.2 ms an item serial); 86 s at 20,000 items becomes seconds.
6. The put cost (31 ms flat: the file write) and the definition clone need nothing at these sizes.

## Limits
- Indicative: one machine, no gate, other sessions; the plan numbers at 5,000 and 20,000 are single plans (one warm sample), the cold ones one each. The 5,000 and 20,000 sets have no brush items; the brush-specific numbers (lists, first-frame cost) are at 100 and 1,000 items only, and a set with brushes at 20,000 would be several times worse for the lists.
- The edit-to-visible latency is the smoke form of the scene at one altitude and one size, measured at 100 items (345 ms against the 300 ms budget; not compared with the world without items) and not completed at 1,000 items (the exact state did not come within the 40 s of the smoke run: each plan takes 4 s and an edit needs several).
- The editor's frame is measured with the pointer at the middle of the screen and the viewport at 2560x1440 over the plan's wanted tiles (the real window's differ by what its renderer has resident).
- The ceiling and walk stage times are from a temporary timer (not kept); the failure of the proof was not isolated to a condition (a wide cap near an item, or the visit cap).
- The cluster is a disc of 25 km round one place; a planet-wide authored world (every cell dense) would multiply the field's cost per cell, not the plan's reach, and was not measured.
