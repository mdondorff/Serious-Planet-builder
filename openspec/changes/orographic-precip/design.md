## Context
- Today: `climate_from_elevation(seed, d, elevation, interior)` is pointwise: temperature from latitude and height, precipitation `bands(lat) * (1 + 0.45 noise - 0.25 interior)`. `finish_sample` serves both the legacy field (`sample`) and the class-source field (`class_sample`, GEN-115), which the production bake uses. The bake writes four channels per face grid (continent, elevation, temperature, precipitation); `GlobalSample` and the store format have no more.
- The class source (`ClassSource::eval`) is dear per point (the province lattice of `lithology-k` is built on 256² nodes a face for that reason); it gives `e0`, the base elevation before the hill band, and the terms of the plate mask.
- The class weights (GEN-095) read `e0` and the terms; arid rules (GEN-128) will read aridity, so the weights must not feed the climate back: the sweep reads `e0`, never the class-blended base.

## Decisions
1. **Object.** `Orography` is built once from a `ClassSource` (like `ProvinceLattice`): lattices per cube face in memory, read bilinearly at a direction (continuous across face edges, the same geometry as the class lattice), never stored. Outputs at a direction: `precipitation_mm(d)` (annual, the two seasons summed), `aridity(d)`, `seasonality(d)`, `wind(d, season)`. `BakeInputs` holds it next to the class lattice; `class_sample` takes the precipitation from it.
2. **Resolution.** Start value 512 cells a face edge (about 20 km a cell; 1.6 M lattice points, each one `ClassSource::eval` for `e0`: about the cost of the lithology lattice times 6). The draft's 2,048 is 16 times dearer and resolves nothing a belt of 125 to 560 km width needs; the owner's preview round can raise it. A separate 64²-a-face lattice holds the land-sea heating term (decision 4).
3. **Wind** (analytic, per season `s`): the zonal component by latitude band, trades (to about 30°) easterly, westerlies (30° to 60°) westerly, polar easterlies beyond, smoothly blended; a meridional component towards the thermal equator in the trades and poleward in the westerlies; the band edges shift by the seasonal migration (start value about +/-12° with the sun: the summer hemisphere's bands poleward); a low-frequency noise (`fbm`, `libm`, integer hash) turns the direction by up to about 25°; steadiness `= |mean wind| / mean |wind|` over the two seasons, in `[0, 1]`.
4. **Land-sea heating** (monsoon). A smooth land fraction `L(d)` (the class source's land depth, averaged over about 1,000 km on the 64² lattice) gives its gradient; in summer an onshore term (towards higher `L`), in winter an offshore one, scaled by `cos(latitude)` and added to the wind vector (start value: up to 6 m/s, `MONSOON_MS`). It pulls moist air into large continental interiors in summer and away in winter.
5. **Precipitation model** (Smith and Barstad's 2004 physics, solved as a fixed-iteration advection with rain-out instead of in the Fourier domain: their solution is for one steady wind over one plane, and a topological upwind order does not exist where winds converge, rotate or reverse (the ITCZ, a monsoon, easterlies meeting westerlies)):
   - state per lattice cell: the water vapour column `q` and the cloud water `c`, both in mm of water; per season a static wind field (decision 3 and 4);
   - one iteration is a Jacobi step: every cell reads the previous iteration's lattices only, so the result does not depend on any cell order or thread count (CON-14); the displacement of a step is under one cell (`dt = dx / u_max`, `u_max` 15 m/s, about 1,300 s at 20 km a cell), the vapour and the cloud are carried by a semi-Lagrangian step (the departure point is the cell's direction moved back along its own wind, read bilinearly by direction across face edges);
   - sources and sinks of the step, per cell: the vapour relaxes towards saturation `q_sat(T) = 25 mm exp(0.07 (T - 15))` (T at sea level) over the sea with the lag `tau_e` 40,000 s and towards 0.55 of it over land with the lag 60,000 s (recycling); frontal rain of the zonal bands (`rain_bands_mm`, shifted 5 degrees with the season) falls in proportion to the vapour present; forced condensation moves vapour to cloud at the rate `0.18 (w / 2,000 m + convergence)` with `w` the upslope wind (the derivative of the base elevation along the wind times the speed, less a threshold of 0.02 m/s) and the convergence `max(0, -div u)`, bounded by the condensation lag `tau_c` of 1,000 s; the cloud falls out with the lag `tau_f` of 1,000 s (rain `= c (1 - exp(-dt / tau_f))`), which puts the rain on the windward slope shifted by `u tau_f` downwind and leaves a shadow where the vapour is spent;
   - converging winds are handled by the advection itself (vapour piles up where the flows meet and the divergence term lifts it); reversing winds are two seasons, each with its own steady field, so no cell has two directions at once;
   - the run starts from `q = q_sat` over the sea and the recycled share of it over land and takes `K` iterations (start 96, about 1,280 km of travel at 10 m/s; the rain of the last 8 is averaged), so the fields settle to a steady state within the travel time; a test pins that more iterations change the annual `P` by under 2 %;
   - a base frontal term `B(lat)` (today's bands, scaled by the cell's `q / q_sat`) keeps the zonal structure and the interiors' drying; the two seasons' totals are summed to the annual `P`; seasonality `= |P_summer - P_winter| / (P_summer + P_winter)`; the annual-wind comparison runs the same model with the mean wind and no seasonal shift.
   - the class loop is broken by direction: the model reads `e0`, the class source's base elevation before the hill band and before the class relief driver `k` (GEN-096), a function of the plates and the coast alone. Class weights, and so `k` and the carve, read `AI`; `AI` never reads them. Structurally: the builder takes a `BaseElevation` trait (`e0(d)` and the land depth, implemented by the class source and by synthetic test fields), so it cannot see a class weight; the synthetic ridge of the tests uses the same trait.
6. **Aridity.** `AI = P / PET`, `PET = 300 + 45 T` (T the annual mean, SPIKE-81), clamped at `PET >= 150`; no new channel in the store: the bake keeps temperature and precipitation channels, and anything that needs `AI` or seasonality reads the lattice (the class rules, the preview) or computes `AI` from the two channels.
7. **Keys and versions.** `OROGRAPHY_VERSION` (the formulas and constants) and the cells a face edge of the lattice are folded into the class-source identity of the global store (`BakeInputs::climate_id`); `GLOBAL_VERSION` 7 re-keys everything else; stores of version 6 are refused. The layer keys do not read the climate and do not carry it. The legacy `sample` keeps its formula (no class source). A debug build reads `PLANET_OROGRAPHY_CELLS` (`.cargo/config.toml`, 48) for tests; its cells are in the key, so a test lattice never shares a key with the production one, and a release build ignores the variable.
8. **Cost.** 1.6 M cells x 2 seasons x 96 iterations of four bilinear reads and a few `libm` calls, plus 1.6 M `eval` calls for `e0`: the preview of seed 14 at 512 cells takes about 30 s on 8 threads, at 1,024 cells about 65 s more.
9. **Preview** (GEN-130): the numbers that decide it come with the maps: for each belt crossing (lattice nodes with the plate core term above 0.6, the wind component across the belt over 2 m/s) the ratio of the precipitation 100 to 300 km on the windward side to that 100 to 300 km on the lee side, with the median, the p10 and p90, the share of belts above 1.5 and above 3, against the references (Cascades and Sierra Nevada about 2 to 4, southern Andes 5 to 10, Himalaya 3 to 5, so a median of 1.5 to 4 is the start target); the same ratio for a synthetic ridge in the test (above 1.5). Maps of P with the annual wind and with two seasons side by side, their difference, aridity (classes at the UNEP breaks 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 0.65), seasonality, wind arrows at the 30° and 60° bands in both seasons, the land-sea term; the four preview regions' material colours (the look's precipitation colour of the shading code, drawn top-down from `P` and `T`, labelled analytic, not the render); Earth's arid share against the map's. Labels as in GEN-122: an input, not terrain.

## Risks
- The departure point of a step crosses face edges: it is read by direction (`direction_to_face`), not by face indices; the Jacobi step needs the previous lattices of all six faces, so the six faces advance together.
- The base elevation `e0` has no hill band and no carve: rain shadows come from belts (125 to 560 km) only; small ranges and hills are below the lattice.
- Cold-current coastal deserts (Atacama, Namib) need ocean currents: not here.
- Material colours change planet-wide (the owner's look).

## Open for the owner
- Wind band edges, seasonal shift, monsoon strength, `tau`, `kappa`, the base frontal share: set in the preview round.
- Whether the two-season total should keep today's annual mean near Earth's (about 1,000 mm over land) by a normalisation.

## Start values after the first preview (seed 14, 512 cells)
- Wind bands: trades 8 m/s easterly at 15 degrees, westerlies 12 m/s at 45, polar easterlies 5 at 78; the wind bands shift 12 degrees with the season, the rain bands 5 (the dry belts stay dry); a monsoon term of up to 6 m/s; condensation efficiency 0.18; an upslope threshold of 0.02 m/s; 96 iterations of 1,300 s; the land recycles up to 0.55 of its saturation column with a lag of 60,000 s.
- Land mean precipitation 1,054 mm (annual wind alone 689 mm); dry land (aridity under 0.5) 10 % of all land and 20 % of the land warmer than 5 degrees C (Earth's: 30 to 40 %); the subtropical bands are 36 % dry; the equatorial bands are wet (2,900 to 3,100 mm).
- Windward over lee ratio across belts: median 1.62 at 512 cells and 1.53 at 1,024 (25 crossings), inside the start target of 1.5 to 4 and under Earth's references; the first lever is the condensation efficiency, whose rise wets the tropics too.
- The equatorial convergence line (the thermal equator, 12 degrees either side with the season) draws a thin rain line on a landmass: a start-value artefact the owner can judge.
- The test hook: `PLANET_OROGRAPHY_CELLS` (`.cargo/config.toml`, 48) gives tests a coarse lattice; a release build ignores it.
