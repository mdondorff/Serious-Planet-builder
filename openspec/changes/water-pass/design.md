## Design

**Water on the terrain's grid.** The water pass draws the terrain tile's own mesh again (same vertex buffers, same instance index, so the same node record) with a second vertex stage that calls the terrain's: `terrain_vertex` (split from `vs` in both tile formats, behaviour unchanged) returns the morphed local position and height; the water stage moves the vertex to the water level, `local + up x (W - h_morph)` with `up` formed from the node's tile-local numbers (CON-11), adds the wave displacement and passes the undisplaced local position and the vertical depth `W - h_morph` to the fragment stage. The fragment stage discards depth <= 0, rebuilds the waves from the interpolated local position (so the normal is per pixel), reads the copy of the scene target at its own pixel (the seabed, already pre-exposed) and shades. Skirts come with the mesh and close the water's cracks as they close the terrain's.

**What the waves are.** Four plane waves per liquid in the planet-fixed frame (tetrahedron directions), so that two tiles share one phase function: the CPU reduces `(k . O + phi - omega t) mod 2 pi` per tile in f64 (the only place a planet-sized number meets a wave vector) and the shader adds `k . local` and reduces the sum to `[-pi, pi]` before `sin` and `cos`. ADR 0041 point 5 says the wave vector is projected onto the local tangent per tile; with a per-tile projection two tiles disagree about the phase of a shared point by about half a radian at a 10 km tile (`(k_t1 - k_t2) . local`), a seam at every tile border. The shader instead weights each wave by its tangent share at the point (continuous, from the up vector it already forms), which keeps every wave near the surface and every tile consistent; recorded as a reading in ADR 0053. The fade with distance uses the LOD's own spacing-per-distance `rho`, so a wave fades where the grid stops holding four vertices per wavelength, whatever the tile.

**Shoreline.** One scalar per pixel: the depth. The surface layer's cover, the foam band and the discrete class are functions of it, all continuous but the class. The surface never dips below the terrain where the water is thin because the wave amplitude is multiplied by a smoothstep of the depth that keeps the vertical displacement under half the depth.

**Surface.** `seabed x T + body x (1 - T)` with `T = exp(-sigma p)` per channel (`sigma` from the liquid's colour and the look's absorption, `p` the vertical depth over the cosine of the view to the up), `body` the liquid's colour times the same light as the terrain's (the atmosphere's lighting function, or Lambert without), Fresnel (Schlick, F0 0.02) of the sky-view table's radiance along the mirror direction (from orbit, the multiple-scattering ambient), GGX sun glint with the half vector's sine from `|n x h|^2` (no cancellation near the peak) and the sun's transmitted and shadowed light, emission times `1 - T`, the surface layer faded in from the shore, then foam. The aerial-perspective composite hazes it as it hazes terrain because the water writes the one depth buffer.

**Selection (ADR 0051 point 6).** Option 1: the node's tile is asked for its water by 25 queries of W, memoised; no storage, no format change. Measured 3.6 us per new tile, 0.14 us per query on an in-memory bake. The limit (a dry basin in a wet tile is flooded) is recorded; the per-vertex mask (option 3) is the fix if it shows, and the planner decides before it is built.

**The frustum cut.** Found by the coastal flight: terrain tiles whose seabed is below the view's frustum are culled with their water. A field may name a water level; `tile_volume` then also computes the sphere the frustum tests are made with, reaching up to the level; distances, morph intervals and the height bounds are untouched (extending the bounds themselves made the morph interval of fine tiles empty), so no mesh key or other plan moves except in the viewer (the viewer's world names its sea level; the debug path and every test scene do not).

**Coast for a sea level other than 0 (ADR 0051 point 8).** The field's coast stays the continentalness's (item 6). The pass draws water on a wet tile wherever the level is above the terrain: a negative level moves the visible waterline seawards (shelf above the level is dry, the field still says water), a positive level floods the land below it in tiles that hold sea and nowhere else (no flood fill: that needs hydrology). Recorded in ADR 0053; reversible.

**Flicker.** ADR 0041 point 6's metric, made exact: reprojection of each water pixel's ray hit on the sea sphere with the f64 poses, coverage compared in the other frame's 3 x 3 neighbourhood, both directions, holes counted, parallax by terrain excluded by a ground test, near-horizon pixels not judged, on a calm sea; the exact class count is reported. Bands thinner than a pixel alias by sampling (0.24 % exact-class changes on the descent flight, all in the film, foam and shallow bands before the coverage reading was adopted) and are not shading flicker.
