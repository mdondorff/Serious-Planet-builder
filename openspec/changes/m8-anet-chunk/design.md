## Context
Inputs: ADR 0077, SPIKE-33 (`spikes/landscape-evolution/results/results.md`), SPIKE-35 (`spikes/landscape-evolution-2/results/results.md`), the chunk field of `openspec/changes/m8-chunk-field/` (design sections 10 to 16), the stored coupled run (GEN-040, STRM-043). The prototype code is `spikes/landscape-evolution-2/results/landscape-evolution-2-prototype.patch` (never merged): a reference for shapes and for the hash, not code to merge.

## Decisions

### 1. The model (SPIKE-33)
Stream power `dh/dt = U - K A^m S^n` with m 0.45, n 1, K 2e-5 times lithology, U 1e-3 m/yr per 1000 m of base height, dt 1e4 years for the fine steps (the coarse run uses 1e5), solved implicitly (Braun and Willett 2013): `z = (z + dt U + f z_r) / (1 + f)`, `f = K A^m dt / distance`, visiting cells donors-after-receivers. These are the parameters of the stored coupled run too; a chunk reads them from the same `CoupledParams` and `CoupledInput` shape so the two runs agree on what a river is.

### 2. Frozen network
A chunk does no routing and no flood. Receivers and drainage area are computed once at step 0 over the initial fine surface plus the corridor and kept for all N steps (SPIKE-33: with the network a function of position alone, neighbouring chunks agree in their cores). The corridor is the coarse path of the stored run through the chunk: for every coarse cell of the chunk's halo region whose area is over 100 km2 (the river network of ADR 0077), the path from the cell to its receiver, displaced by a noise meander of 700 m (wavelength 0.9 km, zero at the coarse cell centres, points every 40 m), so every chunk finds the same path. Inflow at the halo ring is the coarse drainage area crossing the ring.

### 3. The breach rule (c4, SPIKE-35 item 1)
Three changes together: channel cells (area over 50,000 m2) take no diffusion and no slumping; a cell whose receiver is a higher ring cell is an outlet (no cascade up the channel); inside the corridor a cell drains to the lowest forward neighbour, not the best aligned one. Result on the spike's lattice: 0 uphill channel cells, breach cells 8,000 instead of 20,000, bed 4 m (p50) below its surroundings. "Uphill" means the receiver is higher by more than 1 micrometre.

### 4. Jittered D8 receivers (SPIKE-35 item 3)
The slope of each of the eight directions is scaled by `1 + j (2 h - 1)`, `h` a hash of the lattice cell and the direction (`mix64`), `j` 0.4 (0.4 to 0.8 measured: axis share 0.55 to 0.52 against 0.59 for D8, no extra cost). It is a function of position only, so every chunk makes the same choice, and it is the same function as the coupled run's `jitter_factor`: one definition, shared.

### 5. Halo and step cap
N = 30 (the cap); halo 3 km, the smallest with bit-exact cores at one site (1.5 km leaves 19 cells under 0.5 mm). The stage states the halo in metres and the rings it needs (`ChunkStage::halo_rings`, default step cap plus one; A-net returns 3 km over the cell size), which is at least cap times reach (30 x 8 m). The halo is re-checked at a site with a large river crossing the border and at the final cap (step 2 test, tier B).

### 6. Cell graph or dense raster
`chunkfield::region` builds the ball of cells around a chunk with a hash map. At 8 m cells and a 3 km halo a chunk is 1.9 million cells; the hash map costs about a second per chunk. Step 2 first measures it; if it matters, a dense fast path is added for a chunk whose halo stays inside its face (the usual case), with the graph kept for the face edges and corners (bit-exactness against the graph is a test). The A-net solve runs on the dense array either way, and chunks across a face edge keep the graph's neighbour function.

### 7. Hillslope processes (step 3)
Diffusion (explicit, `k dt / dx^2` within the stability bound, 4 neighbours, fixed order) and slumping (thermal: a cell steeper than the angle of repose gives the excess to its lowest neighbour, Kahn order in cell-index order, a fixed number of passes) on non-channel cells; channels are exempt (decision 3). All `libm`, no unordered containers, no reduction that depends on the thread count (CON-14); one thread per chunk, threads across chunks.

### 8. From change to field
The stage returns the change raster `final - base` on the chunk's raster plus apron; the pyramid, the keys and the manager are the M8-2 ones. Process hash: the parameters of decisions 1 to 7, the jitter seed, the code version.

### 9. Measure, then decide the fade (step 4)
Chunk time per km2 at 4, 8 and 10 m (release, one thread), against SPIKE-33's 0.06 s per km2 at 4 m; the step against the parent at tile levels 11 to 14 at the fixed poses (the table of `m8-chunk-field` design section 11 with A-net instead of the stand-in); the fade time `max |change| / (0.25 m x 60 fps)` for the amplitude measured. Exit criterion 3 says the switch fades in, so the fade is built (`m8-chunk-fade`: a per-vertex change plane next to the compact slots, a node weight, the shader, golden-review); the measurement decides its length and whether a top level above 14 can be used. If criterion 3 fails at every top level, stop and report.

### 10. Viewer
A world with a stored coupled channel enables the chunk stage at runtime (D7): the manager with a half-pool of workers, the disk cache in the store's directory (`PackChunkDisk`), top level 14 until the measurement says otherwise. A world without the channel has no chunk stage.

## Risks and open items
- One site, one seam for the halo; a river crossing the border is the hard case.
- The look is judged from engine renders only at step 5; hillshades of the spike are not the engine.
- Cost of a chunk with a 3 km halo against the 10 s landing budget at a 300 m pan (criterion 3): 5 km core plus 3 km halo at 8 m is about 2 s of one thread by SPIKE-33's rate, at 4 m about 7 s.

### 11. Step 1 as built
`generators::anet`: `CoarseRun` (the stored run's heights, areas and receivers by coarse cell; `StoredRun` over the arrays), `BaseDetail`, `segment_points` and `meander_m` (position-only meander, zero at the coarse cell centres), `Network::build(run, base, lat, region, radius)` giving the corridor cells, their links, the inflow and the initial heights. The corridor is rasterised with half-cell steps, a cell is visited once per run, a run ends where the polyline leaves the region, links join only neighbouring cells and the smallest cell by key wins at a confluence, and every cycle left is cut at its largest cell. The segment set covers the coarse cells the region touches plus one ring. The coarse upsample takes taps past a face edge through the neighbouring face. Open (GEN-049): the inflow carries only the area of river segments (above 100 km2), not of smaller tributaries whose catchment lies outside the region; the bit-exact halo test decides whether that matters.

### 12. Step 2 as built and what it found
- **The solver** (`anet::solve`, `ANet: ChunkStage`): receivers on the base surface (the corridor's links, else the steepest jittered neighbour down, `coupled::jitter_of` shared with the coupled run), Kahn order seeded in cell-key order, the breach (a receiver is cut to at least 0.002 below its donor; a fixed receiver stays and its donor becomes an outlet), drainage area summed over donors in key order, then N implicit steps. The incision starts from the **base field's** surface and the change is `final - base`: the coarse run's heights do not enter the surface (the run gives the network), so there is no large-scale offset to pop in at the top level.
- **Not ported from the prototype:** the SPIKE-35 hash is not reproduced (the prototype is planar, with its own polyline corridor, Perlin noise and a 240 m corridor band); the corridor here is the one-cell path of GEN-047 and the receivers of its cells are its links. The hillslope processes are step 3.
- **Finding: the implicit step couples a channel to the heights of the ring far up the channel.** `z = (z + f z_r) / (1 + f)` with `f = K A^m dt / distance` is about 7 for a river of 200 km2 at 150 m cells and about 100 at 8 m, so a height change at the ring reaches `f / (1 + f)` per cell up the channel: on the synthetic scene a halo of 40 rings against 60 left 426 of 1024 core cells different by up to 15 m. No halo up to the size of a river's reach gives bit-exact cores (SPIKE-35's 3 km result was one site with weaker channels).
- **Decision: anchors.** The corridor is held at the coarse run's height (interpolated along the segment, the lower of it and the base) at every quarter of a coarse segment, so every channel chain meets a fixed cell within about 1.2 km. With the anchors, halos of 40 and 55 rings (6 km at 153 m cells) give 0 differing core cells against 70 rings, 20 rings give 6 cells up to 1.7 m (catchments longer than the halo on the synthetic hills), 10 rings 166 cells up to 15 m. The real halo is chosen with real data in step 4 (tier B check at a river crossing).
- **Cost** (`the_cost_of_a_chunk_at_8_m_cells_and_a_3_km_halo`, release, one thread, synthetic hills, one river): 1.84 million cells, region 0.67 s, network 0.60 s, whole compute 2.7 s; about 3.4 s per chunk of 24 km2 (0.14 s per km2 of core). The hash-map region is 20 % of that, above the tenth of design decision 6: the dense in-face fast path is worth building. At 4 m cells the region is 4 times larger (about 14 s per chunk), over the 10 s landing budget: 8 m cells are the default until the fast path and the measurement say otherwise (GEN-052 asks 0.1 s per km2 at 4 m: not met by this solver).
- **Own known answer** (planner, 2026-10-24): the stage's change on the synthetic scene is pinned (`a_chunk_of_the_synthetic_scene_keeps_its_known_answer`), Windows and Linux in CI. Property tests stand in for the SPIKE-35 hash. Watch for knickpoints at the pinned cells in the step-5 renders (a slope break every 1.2 km along a channel); if visible, smooth the pin over a few cells, do not drop it. Fine detail below 8 m stays with the local stage.

### 13. Step 2 after the numerics review
The review found four defects, fixed here:
- **Breach cascade (A).** The breach was a running minimum over the whole chain of any downhill receiver, so on a gentle slope a cell depended on how far the region reached upstream. It now runs only along the corridor's links and only over `breach_window` links (a bounded running minimum, Jacobi iterations over the few thousand corridor cells).
- **Severed rivers at anchors (B).** A fixed receiver above its donor made the donor an outlet, cutting the river's area at almost every anchor. A link to a fixed cell is never cut now: the donor settles up to it (a flat behind a dam), a free corridor receiver above its donor is cut down to it, and only a link between two fixed cells the wrong way, or a donor off the corridor, becomes an outlet. The corridor's area is the coarse areas interpolated along the segment (a function of position), not an accumulation that depended on where the region starts.
- **Catchment area (C).** Drainage area off the corridor is accumulated over at most `area_window` links upstream, so two chunks cut a long catchment at the same distance; and a cell off the corridor takes at most `tributary_f_max` (0.5) of the step. Pure incision with no uplift drives any chain with `f` above about 0.15 to its outlet's height within 30 steps (a flat), so a tributary's profile depends on its outlet: the corridor is held by its anchors, a tributary runs to the corridor (or a pit) inside the region in real terrain. A stream that runs out through the ring without meeting a river is the case that stays region dependent: on the synthetic gentle valley the cores differ by up to 5 mm between halos of 20 and 40 rings (GEN-049 allows 2 cm there), and 0 cells differ on the synthetic hills.
- **Bound (D).** The change is clamped to the stage's `bound_m` (300 m), which the plan's bounds rely on (GEN-039).
- **Definition hash (E).** It covers the stored run's and the base's identity (`CoarseRun::identity`, `BaseDetail::identity`), the windows and the tributary cap.
Calibration of `K`, `dt` and `N` (how deep 30 steps of pure incision cut) is left to the look review in step 5; the clamp and the anchors keep it bounded meanwhile.

### 14. Step 3 as built (hillslopes)
`anet::diffuse` and `anet::slump_deltas` run after each incision sweep on the cells within `steps - t` rings of the chunk at step `t` (the reach a core cell still has), so they do not depend on the halo; diffusion is `z + cf (sum of four edge neighbours - 4 z)` with `cf = min(K dt / cell^2, 0.12)` (0.12 at 8 m cells: the stability cap), slumping is one pass per step of `0.09 (slope - 0.9) distance` from a cell over the angle of repose to the lower neighbour, each cell summing its own eight pairs in the neighbour table's order (so the result does not depend on the region's numbering, and the deltas sum to zero). Channels (area over 50,000 m2), the corridor and fixed cells (anchors, the ring) are exempt: a pair with one of them is skipped. The halo and the known answer are unchanged in kind: the halo test passes with the processes on; the known answer is re-pinned. The talus of 0.9 (42 degrees) and the diffusivity are SPIKE-33's; the look review calibrates them.

### 15. Step 3 after the numerics review
- **Conservation at cube corners.** The region's neighbour table is not symmetric near the eight corners (a cell can list a neighbour that does not list it back), so a pair counted from both sides did not cancel (a 50 m spike lost 13 m). Slumping now adds what a cell gives to the neighbour it goes to, summing each receiver's gifts in order of the giver's cell key, so the sum is zero wherever both cells are covered, and the order does not depend on the region's numbering.
- **Exactness reaches the apron only.** A cell at depth `d` gets `steps - d + 1` hillslope passes (none past depth `steps`), so the values are exact for the core and one ring of apron, not out to `halo - step_cap` as the generic `ChunkStage::compute` contract says; the doc now states that a network stage promises the apron only (the chunk keeps its core, the pyramid its core, so nothing reads further).
- **Not asserted:** that no free cell is steeper than the repose angle after the passes (a cell beside a protected channel never slumps toward it, only diffuses; a cut of tens of metres at 8 m cells leaves a steep bank; the look review decides whether banks need their own rule); the effective diffusivity at 8 m cells is the stability cap (`cf` 0.12 against 0.78 asked: 6.5 times less), so the `diffusion` parameter stops mattering above 0.0008 m2 per year there; the effects are for the calibration in step 5.
- **Guard:** a region with a halo under 2 rings runs no hillslope passes (it has no neighbour tables to read).

### 17. Anchors relative to the drawn ground (owner decision 2026-10-25, terrain relief into M8 part 1)
- **Finding** (learnings 2026-10-25): the anchors of section 12 (the coupled run's height, the lower of it and the base) lie 1.2 to 2.7 km under the base at the review sites, because the run compresses relief against the base; the solve cut to -2659 m and -1322 m, 15,000 cells sat at the 300 m clamp, and every channel became a vertical 300 m slot (ADR 0074 caps its tiles).
- **Decision: the law.** Anchor height at a quarter point of a coarse segment = base height there - `d(A)`, `d(A) = min(cap_m, depth_m (A / area_ref_m2)^exponent)` with `depth_m` 3 m at `area_ref_m2` 1e8 m2 (the river threshold), exponent 0.5, `cap_m` 100 m (a 100 km2 river 3 m, 10,000 km2 30 m, 100,000 km2 the cap), `A` the river's area interpolated along the segment (the run's, as `river_area_m2`). The run's height sets no anchor; the run still sets the network, the areas and the initial surface.
- **Profile.** The anchors of a segment are the running maximum from downstream of the raw anchors (base minus depth) of its own points and those of the next `window` (8) segments down the river (each segment's points are a function of position alone), so an anchor is not under the raw anchors below it within the window; the maximum, not the minimum: a minimum would carry a deep valley downstream up the river and bring the runaway back. Limits (numerics review): a sliding maximum is not monotone across a segment boundary, so a ridge nine segments down can raise the fixed cell one quarter segment below a boundary by the rise beyond the window; and where an anchor lies above the base the cell stays at the base (the stage takes the lower), which can leave a fixed pit on a dip with a higher anchor downstream. In both the bed climbs a few metres to tens of metres over about 1.2 km (a dam or a flat behind the higher anchor); the links come from the geometry, so no river is severed.
- **Bound.** An anchor is at most `cap_m` (100 m) under the base, so the cap is a third of `bound_m` and no anchor can reach the clamp; the breach and the incision between anchors cut deeper locally (the real chunks: -79 m and -130 m at the two review sites, 0 cells at the clamp, 8,786 and 14,842 cells cut over 20 m).
- **Calibration.** The constants (3 m, 0.5, 100 m, window 8) are first values for the look review; the law is a function of area only (no slope, no uplift).
- **Cost.** The anchors read the base at 5 points of every segment the region touches and of up to 8 segments down the river, in one batch (at most about 750 points per chunk against about 1.8 million region cells): a second, small base read per chunk, which the next perf window's base-field time shows.
- **Version.** `ANET_VERSION` 2 and the law's bits in the definition hash re-key every chunk; the synthetic known answer is re-pinned.

### 18. The bank rule (planner decision 2026-10-25; ANET_VERSION 3)
- **Finding** (learnings 2026-10-25, walls): the hillslope steps skip every pair with a protected cell, so the bank next to a channel, corridor or anchor cell is never relaxed: after R-a 6,010 of 1.48 million edge pairs (steepest site) and 20,612 (second site) were over the angle of repose 0.9, 42 % and 22 % of them with a protected cell, and a stronger slump rate (0.5) or 90 steps left those pairs at 2,200 to 2,700 (the rate also overshoots: 0.5 makes the free pairs oscillate).
- **Decision.** After the incision steps, the free cells are settled to the lower cone of slope `talus` (0.9) from every sink, a protected cell that is not in the outer ring (channel cells, the corridor, anchors): `z(c) <- min(z(c), min over sinks s of z(s) + talus x distance(c, s))`, by a frontier relaxation over the cell neighbours for at most `bank_rounds` (48) rings. The sink cells do not move (the material leaves with the channel); a cell is only ever lowered; the outer ring is not a sink and is skipped as before. It is the limit of the slump rule with an unbounded rate, as an exact minimum, so it cannot overshoot.
- **Reach.** A cell is changed only within `bank_rounds` rings of the core, by sinks at most one ring further out, and the reach is read from the stated halo (`min(bank_rounds, (halo_rings - 2) / 2)`), so a core cell is the same for any region at least as wide (the halo test passes, also at 30 and 70 rings); `bank_rounds` is `ceil(bound_m / (talus cell_m)) + 6` (48 at 8 m: 42 rings of the bound over the repose slope, 6 spare), so any wall the incision can cut is reached; a base cliff steeper than that, never touched by the incision, is cut at the reach and by the stage's clamp (a wall of the excess survives). A production halo is at least `2 bank_rounds + 2` rings (375 against 98).
- **Measured** (real chunks, `chunk_incision`): pairs over 0.9 6,010 to 654 (steepest site; with a protected cell 2,516 to 150) and 20,612 to 4,530 (second site; with a protected cell 4,474 to 68); the steepest pair of two unprotected cells 2.33 to 4.81 and 3.37 to 6.19 (the no-wall-under-a-bed limit leaves a step between a limited free cell and its free neighbour). Capped plan nodes at the stills' poses 126 to 131 to 122 to 126; of the capped tiles of the 30 m pose those with a steepest 8 m slope over 0.9 fall from 22 to 11 (over 3.42 from 10 to 1; the snap point moved 100 m, so the tiles are not the same): the capping that remains is by the neighbourhood's height extent (flat tiles next to a gorge), not by slope.
- **Review fixes** (numerics review): a cell is never lowered under a protected neighbour by more than the repose slope (the settle took the minimum over all sinks and had made 28 new walls below a higher bed on the synthetic ridge; the price is that a free cell between a high bed and a low one stays over the low one's cone: 4 of 194 bank pairs, 2 %, on the synthetic ridge); the reach reads the stated halo (it read the region's); the seeds go one ring beyond the settled area, not twice.
- **Not done.** Pairs of a channel cell and a free cell that is lower than it (a bed held up behind an anchor) are not banks and stay; the pairs of two free cells over 0.9 that remain (570 and 4,566 less those with a protected cell) are the slump rule's rate limit.
