## Context
- The bake is a lattice of 16,384 cells a face edge (611 m). `read_direction` blends the four corner samples of a cell bilinearly (ADR 0027): continuous, with a slope jump across every cell edge and saddles inside a cell.
- From 300 m the jumps read as pyramid-like facets where the base is steep; on flat ground the crease detail (amplitude about 100 m, always carving) hides them (E2b probe, 2026-11-03).

## Decisions
1. Interpolant: Catmull-Rom, tension 0.5, separable (rows, then columns).
   - It interpolates: a lattice point is its own sample (weights `(0, 1, 0, 0)` exactly), so coasts, continent shapes and every stored sample stay where they are.
   - C1 across every cell edge; small overshoot beside steps and spikes (at most 0.119 of the local sample range in the tests; bound 0.28125, see 4).
   - Rejected: smoothstep weights (zero slope at every node gives terraces); a quadratic or cubic B-spline (no overshoot, but it does not interpolate: every peak and coast of the bake lowers or moves, and the calibrations made on the bake shift).
2. Face edges and corners.
   - The stencil of a point in the last or first cell of a face edge reaches nodes one cell beyond it. Such a node is the bilinear read (`read_direction`) of the direction of the extended face coordinates, that is the neighbour face's own samples (at a cube corner the third face's, on the diagonal beyond both edges).
   - Across a face edge the two faces' lattices are warped differently, so the ghost does not sit on a vertex of the neighbour: the slope break across a face edge falls about threefold (tests: mean 161 m per cell against 459 for bilinear), it is not removed. A second pass reading the ghost by C1 gave no gain and is not used.
   - Same bits from either face: the read is a pure function of the direction; edge points and corners given in either adjacent face's coordinates read the same bits (tested on 371 directions on all 12 edges, near the corners, and on the 8 corners).
3. A field beside the old one, not a replacement. `cpu-erosion-v2` stands beside `cpu-erosion-v1` with a new key family (inputs hash with `EROSION_C1_VERSION` and `READER_C1_VERSION`), selected by `world.height_field`. The default switch, and with it every pin and golden of the default field, is a follow-up after the preview.
4. Bounds (proven in the interior of a face, partly test-validated near a face edge; the numerics review's argument).
   - Weights: a C1 value is a combination of 16 samples whose weights sum to 1; the positive weights sum to at most 1.125^2 + 0.125^2 = 1.28125, so the value lies within 0.28125 of the 16-sample range beyond each end (exact).
   - Window. In the interior of a face (the reach rectangle at least 3 cells from every edge) the scan window is the stencil's own span: one cell below the cap's first cell and two above its last, so it holds every node of every stencil of every cap point (checked on the off-by-ones: no clamp acts). Within 3 cells of an edge it takes a pad of 3 cells, for the neighbour's ghosts. The earlier wide window everywhere made the bounds 5 times v1's for a 40 m cap and the LOD planner capped 43 nodes of the belt-flank view (the still came out soft).
   - Range and steps are taken over every scanned face together (a ghost is a value of the neighbour; the first version took them per face and missed heights by hundreds of metres in the last cell before an edge), the range widened by 0.28125 of itself on each side and by the rounding of the value to f32.
   - Slope. Inside a face the derivative of the Catmull-Rom surface along an axis is at most 1.5 times the largest of the three differences it spans (`sum |a_k|` = 1.5 at t = 0.5, tight), times 1.25 for the four rows: **1.875**. The factor used is 2.25.
   - Near a face edge: a ghost lies exactly one cell inside the neighbour and at most one cell along the edge from the edge node, so its step is bounded by the neighbour's perpendicular plus parallel steps: 2.125 at an edge and 2.157 in a corner cell in the sum form `du + dv` the bounds use (2.25 keeps the margin). This needs the two copies of an edge node on the two faces to be equal (the bake guarantees it, GEN-014). The first version of this note said "twice the neighbour's largest step"; that premise was wrong (the neighbour's axes can be swapped), the conclusion 2.25 holds.
   - The erosion bound's Lipschitz term takes the factor through `max_du`, `max_dv`: `|dE/dtheta| <= res * (max_du + max_dv)`, so the gradient stencil of +-3 cells is bounded, and the second `scan_base` call (the cap plus the stencil's reach) covers the stencils' own windows.
   - Not proved, test-validated only: that the pad-3 windows near an edge cover every neighbour cell the ghosts read (zero slack at a cube corner: any change to `C1_PAD_CELLS` or the coordinate code can break it), the rounding of ghost values (they are the neighbour's f32 reads), and the field-level pad's cover of the range-side rounding is not relied on any more (the scan adds it).
   - Excluded: a bake of fewer than 4 cells a face edge (a ghost at res 2 lies in a face the scan does not visit): the scan declines there (global range), the reader still accepts res 2.
   - Tests: random caps, edge and corner caps, caps in the last cell before an edge over a step lattice (res 64, 16384), caps of 10 m to 3 km beside a step, a spike line, a ramp and noise, and the base alone against `ErosionField::base_bounds` (the creases' slack hides a base miss in the field-level check), plus the f32 rounding peak. The test is sensitive: it fails with a slope factor of 0.5 and below.
5. Cost. The read touches 16 nodes instead of 4 (the gradient stencil adds six reads): about 2.1x on a single `height(dir)`, 1.15x per tile (a per-thread cache of the 4 x 4 stencils, used only in the batch paths and bypassed by the footprint pass: same values, same arithmetic, same bits), 1.4x on `height_bounds` (PR body table).
