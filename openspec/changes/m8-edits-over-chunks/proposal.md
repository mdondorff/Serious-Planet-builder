## Why
M8-7 (D3, owner 2026-10-26): an edit applies under the erosion; it shows at once, its eroded result follows within 10 s (criterion 6). Today an edit never reaches the chunk stage: chunks sit under the stamps (GEN-039 `OnTop`), the chunk key is `(face, i, j)` and a ready chunk is never re-requested. `Under` is representable in the key and refused by `check_chunk_stages`. Two functions answer "which tiles does an edit change" (`editor/feed.rs`, `frame/lod.rs`) and none answers it for chunks; the horizon bake re-bakes every chunk on any edit (`spec_hash` folds the definition revision).

## What Changes
Step 1, R3 (refactor, no behaviour change, own PR): one function in `world-def/footprint.rs`, `edit_reach`, taking an edit's `(dir, reach_m, strokes)` and a target (a tile cap with a halo in tile edges, or a chunk square with a halo in metres) and answering whether it can change it. `feed.rs::edited_tiles`, `lod.rs::change_reaches_*` and the chunk side use it; tier A: equal sets to today's on the existing brute-force scan tests.

Step 2, the chunk side (this change):
1. **Which chunks an edit re-keys.** The A-net input becomes base plus the stamps (`WorldBase` reads the stamps under the layer setting `Under`). A chunk's version is its key plus the footprint hash of the items touching its square plus `ANET_HALO_M` (3 km), the same hash the tiles use (`footprint_hash`). An edit changes the version of every chunk whose square plus halo its reach touches, for the old and the new item. A small stamp (reach under about 1.9 km) touches 1 to 4 chunks; 5 km about 9 to 16; the 40 km maximum 400 and more (my arithmetic, not measured).
2. **Order and cap.** Stale chunks are not computed eagerly. A touched chunk keeps drawing its old version (STRM-054) and is re-solved when the plan reads it, nearest to the camera first, ahead of prefetch (STRM-042), within the 3 workers and the 250-chunk cap. So a 40 km stamp re-solves what is in view, the rest when it comes into view. While a brush stroke or a handle drags, the re-solve starts 500 ms after the last change (debounce; the preview of VIEW-023 carries the drag); one version per chunk is in flight, a newer edit queues behind it and replaces the older request.
3. **The look of the eroded result.** The edit shows at once as the stamp over the old chunk change (as today). The new version lands as a second landing: the geometry fades by the increment plane over the replaced tile (STRM-052 to STRM-054, about 1.5 s). The drivers (soil, moisture) switch at the swap: the known limit (Tracked, "Driver fade seam"); the driver increment plane is its fix and is not part of this change.
4. **Criterion 6 against the chunk time.** The chunk job is 13.5 s at 8 m (4.5 s without routing; GEN-052 budget 4 s). With 3 workers, 4 chunks are two rounds. For the eroded result within 10 s of the edit (0.5 s debounce, 1.5 s fade, about 2 s refresh and upload leave about 6 s of job time) the re-solve must take at most about 3 s per chunk for a 4-chunk stamp, at most 6 s for one to three chunks. The edit does not change the global network, so a re-solve may reuse the first solve's routing result (the `RouteMemo`, the 8.9 s of the 13.5 s): to be measured first; if it holds, a re-solve is about 4.5 s and one to three chunks meet 10 s. What `m8-routing-cost` must deliver otherwise: at most 3 s per chunk with routing. Larger stamps meet 10 s for the chunks in view only if their count is at most 3 per round; beyond that the rest follow, which is stated, not hidden.
5. **Measurement first** (status, "cost per stamp or brush stroke is M8-7's first measurement"): chunks touched, queue time and landing to drawn for a hill, a 1 km brush stroke and a 5 km stamp on the 16384 store, by the edit-latency scene.
6. **The horizon bake** re-bakes the touched chunks only (the same set), not every chunk.

## Questions for the owner (listed, not decided)
- The global river network and coupled run stay fixed under an edit: a river does not reroute around a new hill. Re-making the coupled run on demand (up to 10 min, criterion 4) is the alternative.
- Disk cache: every edit version of a chunk is a new record (the key holds the footprint hash); old versions stay until the cache evicts them. Accept, or drop a superseded version at once.
- The layer setting: `Under` becomes the default for the edit layer of a world with a chunk stage; `OnTop` stays selectable later.
