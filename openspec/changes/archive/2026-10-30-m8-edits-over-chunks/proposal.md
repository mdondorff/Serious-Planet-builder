## Why
M8-7 (D3, owner 2026-10-26): an edit applies under the erosion; it shows at once, its eroded result follows within 10 s (criterion 6). Today an edit never reaches the chunk stage: chunks sit under the stamps (GEN-039 `OnTop`), the chunk key is `(face, i, j)` and a ready chunk is never re-requested. `Under` is representable in the key and refused by `check_chunk_stages`. Two functions answer "which tiles does an edit change" (`editor/feed.rs`, `frame/lod.rs`) and none answers it for chunks; the horizon bake re-bakes every chunk on any edit (`spec_hash` folds the definition revision).

## What Changes
Step 1, R3 (refactor, no behaviour change, own PR): one function in `world-def/footprint.rs`, `edit_reach`, taking an edit's `(dir, reach_m, strokes)` and a target (a tile cap with a halo in tile edges, or a chunk square with a halo in metres) and answering whether it can change it. `feed.rs::edited_tiles`, `lod.rs::change_reaches_*` and the chunk side use it; tier A: equal sets to today's on the existing brute-force scan tests.

Step 2, the chunk side (this change):
1. **Which chunks an edit re-keys.** The A-net input becomes base plus the stamps (`WorldBase` reads the stamps under the layer setting `Under`). A chunk's version is its key plus the footprint hash of the items touching its square plus `ANET_HALO_M` (3 km), the same hash the tiles use (`footprint_hash`). An edit changes the version of every chunk whose square plus halo its reach touches, for the old and the new item. A small stamp (reach under about 1.9 km) touches 1 to 4 chunks; 5 km about 9 to 16; the 40 km maximum 400 and more (my arithmetic, not measured).
2. **Order and cap.** Stale chunks are not computed eagerly. A touched chunk keeps drawing its old version (STRM-054) and is re-solved when the plan reads it, nearest to the camera first, ahead of prefetch (STRM-042), within the 250-chunk cap. Re-solves of edited chunks hold at most 2 of the 3 chunk workers, so an edit cannot starve the chunks the descent and the flight need (CON-22); a third worker always serves new chunks. A 40 km stamp re-solves what is in view, the rest when it comes into view. While a brush stroke or a handle drags, the re-solve starts 500 ms after the last change (debounce; the preview of VIEW-023 carries the drag); one version per chunk is in flight, a newer edit queues behind it and replaces the older request.
3. **What the user sees in the first 10 s.** A chunk stores its change as a delta over the base (`z - z_base`, bounded), so until the re-solve lands the ground is the new base plus the stamp plus the old delta, drawn the way `OnTop` draws it today: the edit is visible at once, within the M6 budgets (preview 16 ms, drawn-exact 300 ms to 1 s), and nothing waits for a chunk (CON-22). The old delta is slightly wrong where the stamp changed the ground (the erosion it holds belongs to the old ground); that is the interim approximation, replaced by the exact result at the landing. **The eroded result.** The new version (a delta over base plus stamps) lands as a second landing: the geometry fades by the increment plane over the replaced tile (STRM-052 to STRM-054, about 1.5 s). The drivers (soil, moisture) switch at the swap: the known limit (Tracked, "Driver fade seam"); the driver increment plane is its fix and is not part of this change.
4. **Criterion 6 against the chunk time.** The 13.5 s of the earlier measurement was the 256-bake perf scene; on production geometry a cold chunk is 6.8 to 7.5 s (network 3.4 to 3.8 s, of it 2.3 s the region's base heights; routing about 1 s after #730; the rest the solve), and a perf window on the real store follows (explorer). With 4 chunks on 2 workers that is two rounds, about 14 s, over the 10 s of criterion 6 (0.5 s debounce, 1.5 s fade and about 2 s refresh and upload leave about 6 s of job time). So the edit path needs its own reuse, a measured step of this change (step 5):
   - **Base heights.** A re-solve reads the unchanged base heights outside the edit's reach from the first solve, keyed by footprint version (the same rule as the routing memo); the heights inside the reach plus the routing band are computed again. Saves most of the 2.3 s.
   - **Routing.** The routing raster reads the edited base (Under), so the `RouteMemo` cannot be reused as it is: entries inside the edit's reach plus the routing band hold pre-edit heights, and a re-solve would differ from a fresh solve of the same version. The memo is keyed by the chunk's footprint version, or its entries inside the reach plus band are evicted; either keeps the entries outside.
   - **Test, tier A, both reuses:** a re-solve after an edit equals a cold solve of the new version bit for bit (CON-14).
   - **Target:** a re-solve of at most 3 s per chunk (4 chunks, two rounds, 6 s). If the reuse falls short, the measured figure becomes the stated bound (fewer chunks meet 10 s) and the owner is told.
   A routed path meeting a new hill inside the band goes around it (a feature: the corridor follows the new ground).
   **The bound.** Criterion 6 (10 s) is asserted for edits whose touched set is at most 4 chunks (reach up to about 1.9 km): two rounds on 2 workers, at most 3 s each. Larger edits converge nearest-first at ceil(n / 2) rounds of the job time (5 km: 9 to 16 chunks, 5 to 8 rounds, about 15 to 25 s at 3 s a chunk, and only for the chunks in view); this is stated in the spec as the bound, not hidden.
5. **Measurement first, then the reuse** (status, "cost per stamp or brush stroke is M8-7's first measurement"): chunks touched, queue time and landing to drawn for a hill, a 1 km brush stroke and a 5 km stamp on the 16384 store, by the edit-latency scene.
6. **The horizon bake** re-bakes the touched chunks only (the same set), not every chunk.

## Questions for the owner (listed, not decided)
- The global river network and coupled run stay fixed under an edit: a river does not reroute around a new hill. Re-making the coupled run on demand (up to 10 min, criterion 4) is the alternative.
- Disk cache: every edit version of a chunk is a new record (the key holds the footprint hash); old versions stay until the cache evicts them. Accept, or drop a superseded version at once.
