## Design

**Two changes.** Item 5 is a CPU reference plus about six passes with their oracles and goldens. The reference comes first because it fixes the model, the table layouts, the texel mappings and the GPU word layout (`Atmosphere::static_words`, `FrameGeometry::words`) that the passes and the tier-B oracle share, and because its tier-A bounds say how good the model is before any shader exists.

**Model.** Hillaire's 2020 model with the Earth coefficients of the paper. Radiance is in units of the top-of-atmosphere illuminance 1. The sun is a disc of angular radius `r_sun` (look data): its radiance is `1 / (pi r_sun^2)`, and the same radius is the width of the terminator's penumbra: the planet's shadow factor is a smoothstep of the sun's cosine across `mu_horizon +- r_sun` instead of Hillaire's hard test, so a sample never jumps when a camera moves a millimetre (CON-11 v1.4, temporal stability) and a sunset is continuous.

**Tables.** The transmittance table stores optical depth, not transmittance: interpolating a smooth quantity and exponentiating after is far more accurate than interpolating a quantity that falls by orders of magnitude (the measured worst is 2.2 % of T at 63 km, mu -0.14, against 3 %). Axes are Hillaire's (`x_mu` from the distance to the top, `x_r` from the horizon distance), formed with the altitude-relative primitives; texel `i` sits at `i / (N - 1)`, lookups are a manual bilinear interpolation, so the GPU's `textureLoad` path and the oracle share the weights exactly. The multiple-scattering table is Hillaire's `psi_ms = L_2 / (1 - f_ms)` over 8 x 8 uniform directions and 40 midpoint steps, with the ground bounce; the same ground expression (`ground_radiance`) lights the terrain in the passes change. The 32 x 32 table is coarse across the terminator (absolute error up to 6e-4 where the value is 1e-3); the bound says so (ATMO-007) and nothing larger is needed: it is the ambient term.

**Primitives at every altitude.** SPIKE-09 settled it; the module has one set of functions and a `RayOrigin` (`h`, `r`, `c`, `r mu`, `r mu_sun`) from which a sample's altitude, radius and sun cosine are formed. For an orbit ray the origin is the atmosphere's entry point (`h = H`, `c = rho_top^2`, `r mu = -sqrt(D_top)`, `r mu_sun = r mu_sun_cam + t0 cos(view, sun)`): the samples are measured from there, so a camera 20,000 km away does not put 2e7-sized numbers into the sample altitudes. The inside/outside switch is `h < H`, decided on the CPU.

**Sky-view mapping.** Rows: `theta = theta_h (1 - (1 - 2v)^2)` above the horizon (finest at the horizon, where the first row above it is 1.4e-4 rad away at 1 m), `theta_h + beta (2v - 1)^2` below. Columns: the azimuth from the sun `phi = pi u^2` (finest at the sun, where the Mie lobe is). The inverse takes `phi = atan2(|dir . e2|, dir . e1)` with `e1`, `e2` the horizontal axes towards the sun (CPU, f64): `cos(phi)` near the sun is `1 - phi^2/2`, which f32 cannot resolve below `phi` of 3.5e-4, while the two dot products are plain.

**Step layouts.** Sky view: 32 quadratic steps (dense near the camera). Aerial perspective: slice `k` at `256 km ((k+1)/32)^2`, `min(2(k+1), 64)` uniform steps, one independent march per texel (the oracle compares texels one to one). Orbit: 40 uniform steps from the entry. The measured discretisation error against converged marches is in ATMO-007; the worst is the orbit march at the limb at twilight (70 % of a 5 % bound).

**Plan.** The atmosphere is opt-in per plan (a body radius is needed and the plan builder, not the look, has it), so the default plans' text and hashes do not move; the look's `atmosphere` table is data for whoever builds the plan with `with_atmosphere`.

**Look schema 3.** One table, an identity step 2 to 3 (LOOK-002's rule), the version 1 and 2 fixtures kept and loaded through the real chain, a version 3 fixture with integer literals among the numbers.
