# Spike register

Time-boxed experiments that answer a question before a decision. Opened and closed with `/spike`. Spike code lives in `spikes/<name>/` and is never merged into the main crates as-is.

Planned spikes from the background report (open them when their milestone starts):
- GPU-driven indirect terrain/instance path in wgpu. Milestone: M2. (SPIKE-01, closed 2026-10-10.)
- CDLOD morph quality on steep terrain. Milestone: M2. (SPIKE-02, closed 2026-10-10 for smooth terrain; steep terrain remains open for M3.)
- Global hydrology on the sphere (stream-power on a 1–4 km grid + halo-based local carving). Milestone: M3. Fallback: local erosion filter + rivers on a coarse flow grid.
- City HLOD with 10^5 buildings. Milestone: M7.

## SPIKE-07 Finest geometry level
Status: closed
Opened: 2026-10-15
Closed: 2026-10-15
Question: What do the TEST-013 flight frames and the node counts look like with the finest geometry level capped at 22 and 23 (vertex spacing 7.46 cm and 3.73 cm at cells 32) instead of 26 (4.7 mm), with and without a shader detail term below the finest level, and what do height queries (collision, editor picking) use instead of the mesh?
Why it matters: ADR 0033 (the finest level; whether the detail below it is visual only or a field, review item E), owner decision (d) of 2026-10-13, ADR 0031/0033 sizing (nodes and bytes at ground-1m).
Time box: one day.
Success criteria: node counts per case; flight frames for the owner (downscaled contact sheets, full sets in `target/review/`); the detail term's GPU result against a CPU reference within CON-14; height queries confirmed in code; recommendation for ADR 0033.
Fallback if it fails: keep level 26.
Code location: `crates/render/src/spike_detail.rs` (feature `spike07`, off by default, not part of the product), driver `crates/app/examples/finest_level.rs`, checks `crates/render/tests/spike07_detail.rs`. Re-run: `cargo run --release -p planet --features spike07 --example finest_level -- --adapter hardware --frames 240 --caps 26,23,22 --out target/review/spike07` (`SPIKE07_GAIN=10` multiplies the term for looking only); `cargo test -p planet-render --features spike07 --test spike07_detail` (`PLANET_ADAPTER=hardware` for the RTX); node counts per standard view: SPIKE-05's `residency_counts` (`--mem-res 1024`). No production behaviour, golden or MESH_VERSION changed; no golden candidate appeared (the term is a separate shader module and renderer).
Result (2026-10-15, main at cd3243b; eroded field `cpu-erosion-v1` over an in-memory 1024-cell bake, seed 1; TEST-013 flight, 240 frames, 1280x720, relief view, cells 32, tau 6, RTX 3080 Ti Laptop; correctness runs, not perf):
- **Spacing (cells 32, tile edge = quarter great circle / 2^level, at a face middle):** level 26 4.7 mm (tile 0.149 m), level 23 3.73 cm (1.19 m), level 22 7.46 cm (2.39 m).
- **Node counts, flight** (draw calls per frame; max / last frame at 1 m / sum over 240 frames): level 26 2,762 / 2,723 / 307,814; level 23 2,399 / 2,372 / 287,411 (-13 % at 1 m); level 22 2,222 / 2,201 / 275,612 (-19 % at 1 m). Only the last 60 frames (below about 3 m) differ.
- **Node counts, standard views, ground-1m** (SPIKE-05's tool over the same bake; 2560x1440; draw set / surroundings set): cells 32: L26 8,757 / 10,712 nodes (470.5 / 575.5 MB at 44 B), L23 7,206 / 9,161 (387.1 / 492.2 MB), L22 6,633 / 8,588 (356.4 / 461.4 MB); cells 64 at equal spacing: L25 2,690 / 3,273, L22 2,213 / 2,796, L21 2,036 / 2,619. Every other view stops at level 19 or coarser, so no other count changes. (SPIKE-05's table over the stored 16384 bake differs by under 0.1 %.)
- **Look** (frames: [caps contact sheet](spikes/spike-07-caps.jpg): rows level 26, 23, 22 and L22 minus L26 times 10, columns frames 160 / 180 / 190 / 200 at 15 m, 2.6 m, 1.1 m, 1 m; [detail contact sheet](spikes/spike-07-detail.jpg): rows L26 plain, L22 + term, L26 + term, L22 + term times 10 (look only). The full 240-frame sets of all six cases (882 MB) are in `target/review/spike07/`, the times-10 run in `target/review/spike07-gain10/`; neither is kept.) The caps are invisible: against level 26, over the last 49 flight frames (1 m altitude) the largest per-channel difference is 71 of 255 on a handful of pixels, the mean difference per frame at most 0.06 of 255, and at most 1,749 of 921,600 pixels (0.19 %) differ by more than 8 (mean over those frames 281 for level 23, 388 for level 22). The difference image shows only the edge pixels of the 1 m height-contour line, which moves by a fraction of a pixel; nothing else moves. Frames above 15 m are identical (difference 0 to 1). Reason: the field has no content below its finest octave (12 m cells; level 22's 7.5 cm vertex is 160 times finer), so the surface is smooth at the vertex scale at every cap, and the relief view's grid and contours are computed per pixel.
- **Mesh against the pure field** (finest tiles of the last frame, both diagonals of every cell, the field's height at the diagonal's midpoint against the mean of its two vertex heights): level 26 worst 3.0e-5 m (mean 6e-6 m), level 23 worst 3.3e-5 m (mean 1.0e-5 m), level 22 worst 1.8e-3 m (mean 1.3e-5 m; 232 tiles, the worst on a steep one). CON-13's 1 mm bound is vertex quantization, not this deviation.
- **Detail term** (visual only: it perturbs the relief view's shading normal and moves no vertex, depth value or height; a tier-C test checks that the pixels the terrain covers are identical with it on and off, and that with it off the pixels equal the product renderer's). It continues the eroded field's octave series (creases `0.25 - (1 - |n|)^3` of smoothstep value noise, 0.55 per octave) with octaves 12 to 22 (cells 6 m down to 6 mm), the amplitude in the field's `a0 * carve` form from the fragment's own height and slope, each octave fading out when a pixel covers more than 0.1 to 0.4 of its cell. It is not the field's own noise: the hash is 32 bit (WGSL has no 64-bit integer) and the lattice is in position space (`position * freq / R`; the field's is `dir * freq`). CON-11: the shader sees an integer lattice cell and an f32 fraction per octave (split in f64 on the CPU like the relief grid's decade phases), cells per metre and a unit `up`; no planet-radius value; it passes the REND-005 scan. An octave is dropped for a node whose extent spans more than 300 of its cells (never visible there: a tile is about 192 pixel footprints wide). The flight frames were rendered with a first limit of 1,000 cells; the numerics review showed that limit exceeds CON-14 at the largest amplitude (about 1.3e-2 degrees at 1,000 cells), so it was lowered to 300 and the check re-run; the 300 rule only drops octaves on tiles where they are invisible, so the frames are not re-rendered.
- **Detail: GPU against CPU** (RTX; 3 origins including a face edge and near an axis; tile-sized probes of 2.4 m and 600 m, 2,000 each): the f32 shader against an f64 reference that takes the absolute position, as the angle between the perturbed normals: worst 2.8e-3 degrees (CON-14's bound is 0.01), at the field's largest amplitude (980 m), tiles of 2.4, 4.8, 9.5 and 600 m with the probes spread over a cube of half-width equal to the tile edge. Without the cells-per-node rule the 600 m tiles reached 0.13 degrees (f32 loses the lattice position far from the tile origin), and the numerics review emulated 1.3e-2 degrees for a 1,000-cell limit, so the rule is set at 300 cells. Probes within f32 rounding of a crease (a kink: the gradient jumps there, as the field's own crease shape does) have no defined answer and are skipped, 2 to 4 % of the probes.
- **Detail: the look.** At the field's own amplitude the term is a faint mottling (mean difference 0.95 of 255 over the last frames, about 1,900 pixels above 8): the field's law gives millimetres to decimetres of height in these octaves (0.55 per octave while the cell halves). Times 10 it reads as plausible crease-and-gully micro relief that fades with distance (still frames only: temporal stability in motion was not judged). The look is the owner's call.
- **Height queries use the field, not the mesh** (confirmed in code): `HeightField::height(dir)` (ADR 0028 point 1: "every consumer calls `height(dir)`"). The adaptive camera (`frame::controller`: `height_above_terrain` and the clearance floor in `step_once` take a `terrain_height_m` function of the direction), the flight path (`frame::terrain::above_terrain`) and altitude, the plan origins (`frame/src/terrain.rs`, `generators/src/mesh.rs`) and cache-verify all call `field.height(dir)`; no code outside `render` reads a mesh's heights. Editor picking does not exist yet (`crates/editor` is a stub until M6); ADR 0028 names collision and picking as `height(dir)` consumers, so a ray march against `height(dir)` is the design. The finest level therefore changes no collision or picking result.
Recommendation (for ADR 0033):
1. **Cap the finest level at 22 for cells 32 (7.46 cm vertex, 2.4 m tile edge), 21 for cells 64**, if the saving is wanted: 24 % fewer ground-1m draw nodes and bytes (6,633 against 8,757 tiles; 19 % fewer draw calls at 1 m in the flight) with no visible change (0.19 % of pixels differ by more than 8, only at the contour line). Level 23 (3.7 cm) is the cautious choice (-18 %, mesh within 0.03 mm of the field); level 22 is up to 1.8 mm off the field on a steep tile (facets of 7.5 cm; irrelevant for collision, which uses the field). Decide it with the tile format: with SPIKE-06's compact format the byte saving is small (29.8 MB against 39.3 MB for the draw set), so the cap then pays mainly in draw calls and generation time; with the 44-byte vertex it saves 114 MB at ground-1m. Keep 26 only if a later field gains content below about 12 cm (none today).
2. **The detail below the finest level is visual only** (normals and shading, no height): a GPU function of tile-local quantities with no CON-07 field implementation, no change to `height(dir)`, collision, picking, mesh or cache keys, or MESH_VERSION. If it ever has to change heights it becomes a field layer: a CPU reference as the canonical implementation (same 32-bit hash and lattice; the spike's `reference_gradient` is a start), a height bound for LOD selection, CON-14 bounds, and `height(dir)` including it. Not recommended now.
3. **Whether to build the term at all is the owner's call on the frames.** At the field's amplitude it is barely visible; a visible ground-level look needs a gain above 1 (an invented design choice) or real small-scale octaves in the field itself (then they are geometry and the finest level matters again). If built it is a change of its own: the 300-cell rule, a debug view (CON-18), tier-C images, a goldens decision.
Open points: one bake (1024 cells, seed 1) and one flight; the camera stays 1 m above the ground, so steep rock faces at close range were not rendered; the term's GPU cost and temporal stability were not measured; mesh deviation sampled on the last frame only; the amplitude uses the fragment's slope as a stand-in for the bake's; the 32-bit hash is not the field's 64-bit one; the shader receives absolute integer lattice cells (about 1e9) in the node uniform, used only in exact wrapping integer arithmetic but not tile-local in the letter of CON-11 (a production version would send the cell index pre-multiplied by the hash constants as u32, which gives the same hash without a lattice index in the shader); the shader scan does not see uniforms.

## SPIKE-06 Compact tile format
Status: closed
Opened: 2026-10-15
Closed: 2026-10-15
Question: Can a shared grid plus per-tile height (and normal) texels replace the 44-byte vertex, how many bytes per tile does it take, what does it do to ground-1m GPU time, to the morph and the seams, and do CON-11 and CON-13 hold?
Why it matters: ADR 0033 (the tile format, terrain's VRAM share, the slot size of pooled slots), M4 item 7 (compact tile format after pooled slots and indirect draws), review items B and 4 (`docs/background/optimization-brainstorm-review.md`).
Time box: two days.
Success criteria: bytes per tile against the vertex format at cells 32 and 64; ground-1m GPU time of both formats on the hardware adapter (indicative, not `cargo xtask perf`); morph and seam mismatch measured; no planet-radius value in the shader; CON-13's 1 mm at the finest level; images of both renderers compared.
Fallback if it fails: keep the 44-byte vertex (pooled slots of 54 KB / 197 KB).
Code location: `crates/render/src/spike_compact.rs` (feature `spike06`, off by default, not part of the product), driver `crates/app/examples/compact_tile.rs`, checks `crates/render/tests/spike06_compact.rs`. Re-run: `cargo run --release -p planet --features spike06 --example compact_tile -- --adapter hardware --field eroded --view ground-1m [--cells 64 --max-level 25] [--blocks --warm 0] [--dump DIR]`; `cargo test -p planet-render --features spike06 --test spike06_compact` (add `PLANET_ADAPTER=hardware` for the RTX). No production behaviour, golden or MESH_VERSION changed; no golden candidate appeared.
Result (2026-10-15, main at 6161d0e; eroded field `cpu-erosion-v1` over an in-memory 1024-cell bake, seed 1, 2560x1440, tau 6, the SPIKE-05 ground-1m view; RTX 3080 Ti Laptop; GPU times are indicative):
- **Format.** No vertex buffer: the vertex shader derives `(i, j)` or the skirt vertex from `vertex_index`; the index buffer is today's `grid_indices`. Per tile one `R32Uint` texel per grid vertex (1,089 at cells 32, 4,225 at 64; skirts need none) in a pooled atlas: 16 bits of tile-relative height between the tile's own min and max, and two snorm8 octahedral normal components. Position = a biquadratic base surface through nine tile-local control points (corners, edge midpoints and centre of the sea-level shell at the origin's height; `R * dir - origin` rounded once in f64 on the CPU) plus the height along the base normal. The geomorph target (`coarse`, `coarse_height`) is the average of neighbouring vertices exactly as `tile_mesh_field` builds it, computed from neighbouring texels, so neither is stored; skirts are the border vertices pushed along the base normal.
- **Bytes per tile** (`BYTES` lines): cells 32: 53,724 B (1,221 x 44) becomes 4,356 B of texels + a 128 B record (control points, height range, skirt depth) = 4,484 B, **12.0x smaller**; cells 64: 197,340 B becomes 16,900 + 128 = 17,028 B, **11.6x**. Per set, from SPIKE-05's counts (cells 32, finest 26): ground-1m draw set 8,763 tiles 470.8 MB becomes **39.3 MB**; surroundings set 10,751 tiles 577.6 MB becomes **48.2 MB**; finest 22: 6,639 tiles 356.7 MB becomes 29.8 MB. Cells 64 at equal spacing (L25): draw 2,693 tiles 531.4 MB becomes 45.8 MB, surroundings 3,294 tiles 650.0 MB becomes 56.1 MB. A 2 GB mid-tier share holds about 445,000 tiles of 4,484 B (37,000 at 54 KB). **Slot size for pooled slots:** a slot is 33 x 33 (cells 32) or 65 x 65 (cells 64) texels; one 8192 x 8192 texture holds 248 x 248 = 61,504 slots at cells 32 (267 MB) or 126 x 126 = 15,876 at cells 64 (269 MB), so a pool is a few atlas textures with a free list (a texture index per draw, or a texture array: 256 layers by default). The vertex-format alternative needs 54 KB / 197 KB slots in large vertex buffers (the default storage binding limit of 128 MiB holds about 2,400 slots of 54 KB). The 128 B record is per slot; the per-draw node uniform grows from 192 to 368 B (dynamic stride 256 to 512 B: 4.5 MB instead of 2.2 MB written per frame at ground-1m; not attributed in the CPU time, which was unchanged).
- **Model error against the product mesh** (CPU mirror of the shader in f32, every vertex including coarse positions and skirts; the `MODEL ERROR` table): the position error is about 0.018 theta^2 of the tile edge (theta = the tile's angular size) plus f32 rounding: 15 m at level 5, 1.6 cm at level 8, 5e-5 m at level 16, 4e-8 m at level 26. A tile is drawn at about edge/190 per pixel, so the error in pixels is about 3.4 theta^2: under 0.5 px from level 2 and under 0.05 px from level 4. **Levels 0 to 2 (at most 126 tiles) are not good enough** (level 2: 6.9 km, 0.28 % of the edge; 73 pixels of the orbit view differ by more than 32 levels): keep the vertex format for them, or use a higher-order base surface (5 x 5 control points, +192 B per tile). Heights: the error is the quantisation step plus the f32 rounding of the sum, the same rounding as the product's absolute f32 height.
- **CON-13 (1 mm at the finest level): holds for this terrain, it is not a guarantee** (the step is range / 65,535, so 1 mm needs at most 65 m of relief inside one finest tile; a cliff, or cells 64 with a coarser finest level, could exceed it; the jitter half of CON-13 was not run through the compact pipeline). Observed: The quantisation step is range / 65,535 of the tile: 7.0e-9 m at level 26, 1.1e-7 m at level 22, 9e-7 m at level 19, 1e-3 m at level 12 (a 65 m relief range), 3.2e-3 m at level 8 (where the rule does not apply and the step is far below a pixel). A tile whose relief is large against its edge (the steep stress field) is limited by the step and by the f32 error of the base normal (about 2e-7 rad) times the relief; the tier test bounds both.
- **CON-11: holds.** Every shader input is tile-local (control points, heights relative to the tile origin, atlas texel position) or a unit vector; the shader has no planet-radius value and passes the REND-005 scan (`the_compact_shader_contains_no_planet_scale_values`). The fragment stage is the product's own text, spliced at build time, so the two pipelines cannot drift apart there.
- **Seams** (`SEAMS`: one border direction seen by two tiles, world positions, largest mismatch over the ground-1m draw set; product / compact): same level 2.2 mm / 5.5 mm; across levels 1.7 mm / 10.6 mm (the unmorphed even fine vertices were compared; the fully morphed crack follows because a morphed odd vertex lies on the chord between them, it was not measured directly). At coarse levels the compact mismatch follows the model error above (levels 5 to 7: 1 cm to 80 cm; level 2: 83 m), always far under the skirt depth (8 % of the edge) and under a hundredth of a pixel at that distance. It is not zero for equal levels because both tiles share the edge curve but add the height along their own base normal and relative to their own shell. No view showed a hole or a crack the product does not have.
- **Morph.** The morph and morph-height views equal the product's (max channel difference 1; one edge pixel of 3.7 M flips by 69 to 86). The level view is identical on the GPU. Relief view: 625 pixels of 3.7 M differ by more than 8 levels (15 by more than 32), mean difference 0.17 of 255: line pixels of the grid and contours that move by a fraction of a pixel; normals view mean 0.43 (the 8-bit normals). [Relief, aerial-20km: product, compact, difference x 8](spikes/spike-06-compact-relief.jpg).
- **Normals.** Octahedral snorm8: up to 0.9 degrees (0.5 at the fine levels). CON-14 states 0.01 degrees for CPU against GPU implementations of a field layer; the stored normal is a render attribute made from the CPU's canonical normal, not a second implementation, so it needs a reading (point for ADR 0033). If normals must meet 0.01 degrees: two 16-bit components (8 B per texel, 8.7 KB per tile at cells 32, still 6x smaller), or derive the normal in the shader from the heights with a one-texel halo (2 B per texel plus 12 %; not built: more fetches per vertex, and the definition of the normal changes, so goldens and the CPU reference follow).
- **GPU time, ground-1m, 8,687 to 8,757 tiles, RTX, indicative** (timestamp queries, `gpu_ms` median; both formats in one process, 160 to 320 frames each, in blocks and frame by frame, 14 process runs): the first compact shader (a loop over the control points in a uniform array) took 15 ms against 2.9 ms, five times slower: the vertex stage is the cost (96 % of the drawn triangles are hidden, SPIKE-05). Unrolled, with exact neighbours (kept; the CPU mirror agrees with the mesh also on the steep field): **Level view 3.3-3.5 ms against 3.9-4.2 ms (0.8x), Relief 4.2-4.4 against 4.5-4.6 (0.93x)**. With a cheaper morph target (the neighbours' height offsets along the vertex's own normal, `SPIKE06_VARIANT=approx`): **2.7-2.9 against 3.4-4.4 ms (0.65-0.7x) Level, 3.6-3.7 against 4.7 (0.78x) Relief**, but it moves the coarse position (about 0.1 m x slope at level 8, 2 m x slope at level 6) and fails the steep-field tier test (7 m at level 6, cells 4). Cells 64 (2,690 tiles): 2.9 against 3.3 ms; finest level 22: 2.7 against 3.25 ms. CPU encode time is unchanged (6.3 ms). The **same product code reads 2.85 to 6.0 ms** between runs (short bursts boost; sustained load settles near 1.05 GHz, the GPU being half idle in a CPU-bound frame): absolute GPU times from these runs mean nothing, only the same-process ratio does, and the 3.0 to 4.2 ms rise of 2026-10-11/12 may be this effect (`docs/learnings.md`).
- **Generation.** A compact tile costs the same to generate as the mesh (6.1 s each for 8,757 tiles on 20 threads).
Recommendation (for ADR 0033):
1. **Adopt the format for levels 3 and finer, built on the pooled-slot design after the pooled allocator and indirect multi-draw (M4 item 7 order).** It cuts bytes per tile 12x (4.5 KB at cells 32, 17 KB at 64), the ground-1m surroundings set from 578 MB to 48 MB, and it is not slower on the GPU (0.8-0.93x with exact neighbours). The terrain share then stops binding (2 GB is about 445,000 tiles): ADR 0033 can set it from the other content's needs. Pooled slots: `(cells + 1)^2` texels in atlas textures, a 128 B record per slot.
2. Take the **exact** morph target by default; the approximate one only if a perf window shows the GPU is the limit, and then with a tier-B bound test on the real eroded field (the steep field exempt or the interval wider).
3. Levels 0 to 2 keep the 44-byte vertex (or get a 5 x 5 base surface): at most 126 tiles.
4. Settle the normal question (CON-14 reading; 8 or 16 bit; or derived) in ADR 0033 before building; a derived normal changes the CPU reference.
5. The change bumps MESH_VERSION (or a tile-format version) and changes every golden: through `golden-review`. The level and relief views equal the product's on the RTX to a fraction of a pixel; the spike test also passes on WARP for four views at 128 x 96.
Needs a perf window: the ground-1m A/B of the shipped shader under the readiness gate (the clock effect hides the real ratio), exact against approximate at cells 32 and 64, atlas upload time per frame, and the per-draw CPU cost once a texture index per draw is needed.
Open points: one bake (1024 cells, seed 1) and one view for the timings (other views were checked for accuracy and images only); the 5 x 5 base surface and the derived normal were not built; texture binding per draw and the atlas count were not designed (one atlas held every tile of a view); at cells 64 the same law applies (error scales with theta^2 of the tile, so at equal spacing one level coarser).

## SPIKE-05 Residency counts
Status: closed
Opened: 2026-10-15
Closed: 2026-10-15
Question: How many nodes, tiles and bytes (today's 44-byte vertex) does each standard view need resident for (i) today's frustum-cut draw set and (ii) the position-based surroundings set (no frustum cut, horizon cut kept), at finest level 26, 23 and 22 and at cells 32 and 64; how much of what is drawn produces no visible pixel; what does a tile cost to generate; and how long does the plan take, apart from streaming and upload?
Why it matters: ADR 0031 (residency set versus draw set, request order), ADR 0033 (terrain's VRAM share, pooled slots, finest level), ADR 0032 (teleport plan time). Replaces the hand estimates in ADR 0033's context (about 50 KB per tile, 0.4 GB for the ground-1m draw set, 3-6x for the residency set).
Time box: half a day plus an hour.
Success criteria: a table of nodes and MB per standard view and set, at the three finest levels and cells 32 and 64; the morph interval rechecked at 64 on the eroded field; the hidden-triangle share at ground-1m; tiles per second per thread for the eroded field and `cpu-terrain-v1`; plan time reported apart from streaming time.
Fallback if it fails: n/a (counts).
Code location: `crates/app/examples/residency_counts.rs`, a measurement helper and not production code: it calls the production planner (`plan_terrain_cached`), the production selection (`select_nodes_field_cached`) and the production mesh builder, and adds a small CPU depth pass. Re-run: `cargo run --release -p planet --example residency_counts -- --store DIR --global-res N [--mem-res M] --part counts|hidden|throughput` (needs a bake made by `generate --global-dir`).
Result (2026-10-15, main at 5543a6f, one session; the 360-line count listing was `target/spike05/counts.txt`, not kept; the tables below are the result):

Recommendation (for ADR 0031 and ADR 0033):
1. **ADR 0031: adopt position-based residency; it is cheap.** The surroundings set is 1.23x the draw set at the worst view (ground-1m: 10,751 nodes, 578 MB at today's vertex, cells 32, finest 26; 463 MB with the finest level capped at 22) and at most 3x where totals are small (172 MB at aerial-20km); the by-hand 3-6x was too high. It needs no prediction margin for rotation. Size ADR 0033's share against the ground-1m surroundings, 0.58 GB, with the 2 GB and 3 GB starting shares leaving room. A flight also holds the tiles of earlier frames; that union is not counted here and belongs in the residency manager's tests.
2. **Tighten the draw set's frustum cut** (a change to LOD-007, queue it separately): per-tile height bounds instead of the global `max_height_m` pad would cut about two thirds of the ground-1m draw calls with no visible tile lost, more per unit of work than cells 64, and it makes the draw set a true subset of the residency set. Occlusion would remove most of the rest (87 % of tiles have no visible triangle) but conflicts with CON-03; it stays the owner's decision.
3. **ADR 0033: cells 64 is a real option for the CPU** (3.3x fewer nodes, warm plan 5.9 ms instead of 20) at +13 % bytes and a four times coarser tile (35 ms to build); the morph interval holds at 64 on the eroded field. Decide it together with the slot size of the pooled allocator (197 KB at cells 64, 54 KB at 32). A finest-level cap of 22 or 23 saves 18-24 % of the ground-1m tiles and bytes; whether it is worth the look is for SPIKE-07's frames.
4. **ADR 0032: plan time is its own term.** The eroded field's draw plan is 20 ms warm at ground-1m, 180 ms cold, and seconds at altitude over the 16384 bake; none of it is streaming. Origin and mesh key are computed once per resident tile, not per frame, and the plan's cost has to be bounded before a detail-lag budget means anything.
5. **Generation throughput sets the teleport lag, and `StoreSource` is the bottleneck for the production bake.** 1,217 tiles/s (eroded, 20 threads, lock-free source) fills the ground-1m draw set in about 7 s; through the mutex-guarded store source it takes minutes. Fix the source (per-thread block cache or a shared lock-free one; the regional-field stage with halos, ADR 0026 point 5, is the natural place) before the scheduler is measured. GPU evaluation of the local stage (review item 4b) is not yet justified for the draw set by these numbers; it matters for a world-scale teleport.
Needs the architect (perf window): GPU upload time and per-frame upload cost of the draw and surroundings sets on the RTX; CPU encode time per draw at ground-1m (already queued); the plan times above under the readiness gate (these are ad hoc).
Open points: the hidden-triangle pass uses unmorphed positions and the 1024 bake (the 16384 store would take about 40 minutes through the mutex); one bake seed (1); the union of resident tiles along a dive was not counted; the mesh-key explanation of the seconds-long altitude plans is an inference. **Corrected by M4 item 7a (2026-10-15, `docs/learnings.md`):** the ablation shows the mesh key costs 0.00-0.25 ms; the cost is the per-node origin height, and at altitude the store's block cache re-decoding blocks for each coarse tile's centre sample (item 7b).

## SPIKE-03 Global hydrology on the sphere
Status: open
Opened: 2026-10-12
Question: Is global stream-power erosion on the cube sphere (flow routing with depression filling across face edges, drainage accumulation, implicit stream-power steps) practical at coarse grids, how does it compare with the fallback (flow routing and rivers on the unmodified base heights, local carving) in the look of the drainage and in visible seams at face edges and cube corners, and what does it cost in time and memory against the 8 GB budget (CON-21)?
Why it matters: M3's hydrology (SHOULD in the report's MVP; the fallback is the local erosion filter plus rivers on a coarse flow grid); the owner decides which to build. Under ADR 0022 option 1 a multi-step chain is one field, so this needs no same-stage reads; if a design turns out to need them (CON-08), stop and report.
Time box: one agent session.
Success criteria: both approaches run on the same base heights at grids of 256, 512 and 1024 cells per face edge (and a short run at 2048) with times and memory; maps and cube-edge crops of both are rendered into `target/review/hydrology-spike/` with a contact sheet; a seam metric (change of drainage area and of height across face edges against inside faces); an extrapolation to the 4-10 km and 1 km grids.
Fallback if it fails: the fallback itself (local erosion filter plus rivers on a coarse flow grid), which is what the report's MVP assumes.
Code location: spikes/hydrology/ (throwaway; branch `spike/hydrology`, never merged).
Result (2026-10-12, one session; WARP is irrelevant here, all CPU; 20 threads for sampling, ROUTING AND EROSION SINGLE-THREADED; seed 7; images in `target/review/hydrology-spike/`):
- Method. Cell-centre lattice of the cube sphere (res cells per face edge, tangent warp), 8 neighbours, neighbours across face edges and corners through the direction of the extended cell centre (`face_to_direction` beyond the face, then `direction_to_face`); base heights are the pointwise global field at the cell centres. Fallback: priority-flood routing from the ocean (depressions filled by construction), drainage area by accumulation in reverse flood order, rivers where the area exceeds a threshold, the base heights untouched (rivers carved a little for the picture). Global stream power: the same routing repeated, with uplift 20 m per step and the implicit n = 1 update (Braun and Willett, K 31.6 per sqrt(km^2), m 0.5) in flood order, 30 steps; it is one field that reads only the base heights (an earlier stage) and produces heights, receivers and areas, so ADR 0022 option 1 holds and no same-stage reads are needed; a regional tile reading the coarse network for local carving reads an earlier stage's output. NOT TUNED: K, m and the uplift were set for a plausible equilibrium slope, not fitted to a landscape.
- Cost (one thread, release): 256 cells (39 km): 0.03 s per step; 512 (19.5 km): 0.15 s; 1024 (9.8 km, 6.3 M cells): 0.72 s per step, 21 s for 30 steps (routing is 80 %: the priority flood), fallback routing 0.56 s; 2048 (4.9 km, 25 M cells): 3.1 s per step, about 93 s for 30 steps, fallback 2.4 s. Arrays about 44 B per cell (1.1 GB at 2048; the flood's visited, receiver, filled, order and heap add the same again at its peak, not measured). Extrapolation: 4-10 km grids (res 1024 to 4096, up to 100 M cells) take minutes and 2-8 GB; the 1 km grid of the report (16384 cells per face edge, 1.6 billion cells) would take about 3.3 s x 64 = 200 s per step (hours for 30 steps) and tens of GB: global stream power is only feasible on a coarse grid (4-10 km), with the finer scales from local carving; the fallback's routing needs the same coarse grid (its one pass is 30 times cheaper).
- Look. Fallback (left column of the seam sheet): rivers follow the unmodified base topography, with long straight diagonal runs in flat areas (the D8 lattice) and no valleys around them. Stream power (middle): dendritic drainage with valleys and sharpened ridges around the rivers, relief that explains the network; its slopes show lattice-aligned hatching (stair steps along the 8 directions) at 10 km cells.
- Seams. The rivers and valleys of both approaches cross the face edges and the cube corner without a visible step (right column, face edges drawn in magenta). Metric over 4-neighbour land pairs at 1024 cells: stream power, inside faces mean |dh|/dist 0.0038 and mean |dlog10 area| 0.035, across edges 0.0060 and 0.094 (x1.6 and x2.7); the fallback's |dlog10 area| across edges is 0.165 against 0.102 inside (x1.6) and its |dh|/dist is equal (0.0013 against 0.0014). The across-edge excess grows with resolution (256 cells: x1.15 and x1.3). Not resolved in this session: part of it is how a cell across an edge is chosen (cell centres on the two faces are half a cell apart and cells there differ in shape), so a mild seam bias at face edges cannot be excluded; the maps show no visible artefact. Receiver links across face edges: 5434 of 2.9 M land cells at 1024.
Recommendation: for the owner. Stream power gives a markedly better drainage look but only at coarse grids, a 30-step chain that is one global field of tens of seconds to minutes (a cached bake product like the global bake, STRM-012 style), needs hatch-free routing (D-infinity or multiple flow direction, not tried) and a seam check at the real resolution; the fallback is cheap and seam-free by construction but looks plain. A hybrid fits ADR 0022: the global stream-power stage at 4-10 km, local carving and the erosion filter (SPIKE-04, variant B) at the fine scales reading the coarse network from the earlier stage.
Kept images (2026-10-13, downscaled JPEG copies of the contact sheets; the full set under `target/review/` is not kept): [world](spikes/spike-03-hydrology-world.jpg), [seams](spikes/spike-03-hydrology-seams.jpg) (columns: fallback, stream power, stream power with the face edges in magenta; rows: mountain at a cube corner, coast across a face edge).

## SPIKE-04 Erosion filter variants
Status: open
Opened: 2026-10-12
Question: Which pure-function-of-the-direction erosion filter (gradient-damped fBm, slope-incised ridged creases, flow-aligned gullies) gives the most convincing terrain in the relief view over the global bake, at what cost per sample, with what height-bound argument, and does cells 32 / ratio 240 still plan?
Why it matters: M3's local erosion filter (the report's fallback to hydrology, SPIKE-03); the owner picks the look.
Time box: one agent session.
Success criteria: three variants rendered in the relief view at 4 cameras (coast, mountain, 1 m, 200 m) into `target/review/erosion-spike/` with a contact sheet; per variant: ns per sample, the bound argument, plan verdict at cells 32 / ratio 240.
Fallback if it fails: keep the plain fBm detail of `cpu-terrain-v1` (GEN-016).
Code location: spikes/erosion-variants/ (throwaway; branch `spike/erosion-variants`, never merged; it makes `layer_noise` and `layer_noise_with_gradient` public on that branch only).
Result (2026-10-12, one session, bake of 512 cells, seed 7, WARP, release build, one thread; images in `target/review/erosion-spike/`: `contact-sheet.png` = relief view, 4 columns 0-baseline / A-damped / B-incised / C-meander, 4 rows coast-1500m / mountain-2km / ground-1m / hills-400m; `contact-sheet-hillshade.png` = top-down hillshade patches (coast 40 km, mountain flank 8 km, mountain 600 m), same columns, which shows the character far better than the relief view, whose mild lighting makes all variants look flat at flight scale):
- 0 baseline (`cpu-terrain-v1`: fBm of 14 octaves from 64 per radius, persistence 0.45, at most 300 m): 735-760 ns per sample; smooth, no visible erosion character at any of the patches. Measured Lipschitz constant 7.1e5 m/rad (x3 safety).
- A damped fBm (octaves from 256 per radius, persistence 0.55, up to 900 m on land; each octave damped by 1/(1 + (|accumulated gradient, bake slope included| / 1.27e6 m per rad)^2)): about 1.2 us per sample (the bake slope costs 4 extra bake reads). Looks like fine granular rock, rough everywhere, no drainage pattern. Lipschitz 3.0e6 m/rad. Bound argument: weights in (0, 1], so |detail| <= a0 sum(amp)/norm; over a cap the per-octave noise bounds times the weight range, the weight range from the variation of the accumulated gradient (Hessian bound times reach plus the Lipschitz constant of the bake's smoothed gradient): tight on small caps, loose on large ones.
- B incised creases (ridged creases (1 - |n|)^3 per octave, amplitude (80 + 900 land) m scaled by 0.15 + 0.85 smoothstep(slope 0.01 to 0.2) of the bake slope): about 1.2-1.3 us. Branching drainage-like crease network, deeper on steep ground. Lipschitz 1.17e6 m/rad. Bound argument: interval arithmetic: `layer_noise_bounds` per octave, exact monotone map of |n| through (1 - |n|)^3 (the kink of |n| is not a problem for a range), the slope factor from a monotone function of the bake gradient bounded over the cap by cell differences, product of intervals: sound and as tight as the terrain field's.
- C meander (B with the lattice position displaced by 0.8 cells of smooth vector noise at 0.35 of the octave frequency): about 2.7-2.9 us. Meandering, river-like creases. Lipschitz 1.15e6 m/rad. Bound argument: B's, with the noise evaluated over the box the displaced positions can reach (the displacement is Lipschitz, about 2.5 times the box of B): looser at mid octaves, equal for tiny caps.
- A flow-aligned variant was tried first (lattice stretched along the downhill direction) and dropped: the stretch needs a local reference point, and a stretch about the point itself is the identity (the direction is tangent, so the position has no component along it); a real one needs a per-cell reference, which seams at cell borders.
- Planning at cells 32 and viewport / tau 240 (640x360, tau 1.5 px), four cameras, `max_height_m` 10,000: all four fields plan at all four cameras (2.5-9.5 thousand nodes, B and C within 1 % of the baseline, A within 3 %). CAVEAT: the spike's `height_bounds` are Lipschitz bounds about the centre value with the measured constant times 3, not proved, and the bake is 512 cells (not 16384): the ADR 0016 check must be repeated with the real bounds and the production bake.
Recommendation: not made; the owner picks the look from the sheets. Costs: B is about the cost of the baseline plus the bake slope; C doubles it; A and B need the bake's gradient (4 to 8 extra bake reads per sample), which a regional field with a halo would read once per tile instead.
Kept images (2026-10-13, downscaled JPEG copies of the contact sheets; the full set under `target/review/` is not kept): [relief view](spikes/spike-04-erosion-relief.jpg), [hillshade](spikes/spike-04-erosion-hillshade.jpg) (columns 0-baseline / A-damped / B-incised / C-meander, as described above). Variant B was adopted (ADR 0030) as `cpu-erosion-v1`.
