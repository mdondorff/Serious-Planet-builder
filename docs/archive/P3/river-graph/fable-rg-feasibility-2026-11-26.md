# River graph (RG): feasibility and process review for the owner (Fable, 2026-11-26)

Independent, read-only. Sources: the digest (`river-graph/`, `decisions/`, `proposals/`, the morning digests 11-16 to 11-26), the external reviews (`ag_serious-planet-builder/reviews/`, 11-16 to 11-25), the lane order files and outboxes, `origin/main` and `origin/spike/rg-prototype` via `git`. "checked" = read in the cited file; "inferred" = my reasoning. Nothing was run.

## Verdict
1. The river-graph idea is technically sound and partly proven (graph authority, basin objects, partition bit-identity for four stages, wave-1 memory 9.26 GB at full count), but the chain on `spike/rg-prototype` cannot reach the G3 gate as adopted: G3-9 (the owner's look blocker) is attributed 88-93 % to S0/K5, which the plan parks after the port.
2. The development ran in a repair loop: at least 10 fix rows since 11-22 moved the defect to another stage or bar; 5 of 7 look bars registered on 11-24 failed calibration on 11-26; three owner decisions adopted on 11-23/24 were reopened within 48 h.
3. Scope grew from 10 port items (11-20) to 9 rows, 27 risks, 16 owner decisions and 96 chain switches (11-26); the lead's own remaining estimates sum to 8-12 lane-weeks before any port, with the owner as the bottleneck (three flights planned, none flown).
4. Recommendation: **park RG now with a component decision record, keep what is already banked on main, and spend the next effort on the base (K5 hillslope and planform, K7's read), which 4 of 5 flagged defects trace to and which B and RG both need.** Confidence about 70 %.
5. Parking is not sunk-cost avoidance: the gate on main, the planet primitives, the partition proofs, the contracts and the attribution are kept; what idles is the unmerged 6,425-line chain and its 96 switches.

## 2. Feasibility
### 2a. Is the idea sound?
Yes, as an architecture. Checked evidence: graph violations 0 and agreement at 75 km2 94.5-99.5 % on five windows (`g2-report:13-23`); crest p95 0.09-0.44 m against B's 15-316 m, saddles 0-3,423 against B's 40-27,108, measured against S_KH (`g2-report:28-34`); basin ids and spills stable under every partition on the fixture (SPIKE-130, 11-17 digest); KC, band6, base1 and wave 1 bit-identical on five TEST-151 layouts (gate:190, :107); wave 1 at the full landmass count 9.26 GB peak working set against 10.8 GB (crusader outbox 11-26 21:00).
Caveats the lead's documents understate: the preservation claim is against S_KH, and KH itself lowers 10-18 % of land outside every bar; against S0, R3b's saddles are worse than B's (`review-fable-rgv0:96`, `g2-report:32`). Cost is 2.4-3.5x B per window with no planet wall-time bar (O11: reported only).

**Recommendation: C now, then D; E scoped in one lane-day before D starts.** Reasons: (1) the owner's time, not lane time, decides this project's pace, and RG as framed needs the owner on a decision or a flight every day; (2) the look the owner is gating on is upstream of RG, so the correct order is base, then graph; (3) parking loses little that is not recoverable: the contracts, proofs and primitives are on main or tagged, and the plan's own fallback clause anticipated this outcome. Against C: wave-1 memory and the partition proofs are real progress this week, and re-opening later costs a rebaseline and re-learning. I weigh the owner's capacity higher. Confidence about 70 %; B is the defensible alternative if the owner has 3-5 calendar weeks of near-daily attention and accepts the trenches.

What would change the verdict: a flown flight on the fly1126 stores (B, defaults, step 1) where the owner finds step 1's look acceptable with the base defects named. That is 30 minutes and is the cheapest test still unrun.
