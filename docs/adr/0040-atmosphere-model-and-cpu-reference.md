# 0040 Atmosphere: Hillaire 2020 LUTs, CPU reference, and CON-11
Status: accepted (opus-planner, 2026-10-16, under the owner's delegation of 2026-10-12, after SPIKE-09 (#176): point 5 decided as the altitude-relative primitives at every altitude; sketch by opus-planner 2026-10-15, revised after the spec-guardian and numerics reviews; point 5 follows constitution v1.4, ADR 0043)
Date: 2026-10-15
Source: architecture report M5 row (Hillaire LUTs, aerial perspective, "LUTs match the CPU reference within tolerance (tier B)", atmosphere <= 1 ms); owner's M5 decisions 2026-10-15; numerics review 2026-10-15 (worked out by hand, not measured); ADR 0035, ADR 0038, ADR 0039, ADR 0042; constitution CON-02, CON-04, CON-11, CON-13, CON-21

## Context
Hillaire's 2020 model: a transmittance LUT, a multiple-scattering LUT, a per-frame sky-view LUT and a per-frame aerial-perspective volume. Its formulas use the distance r from the planet's centre (about 6.4e6 m) and ray-sphere intersections. In naive f32 at 1 m altitude, r² - R² is about 33 % wrong (r²'s ulp is about 4e6 m² against 1.3e7 m²), which misplaces the horizon and the near-horizon ground distance. SPIKE-09 (2026-10-16) measured it: at 1 m, 10.5 % of rays fail the bound on the RTX (worst 1,286x, ground and sky misclassified up to 5.6e-3 rad below the horizon, the ground distance 20-32 % wrong), and **at 2 km WARP still fails 3 % of rays (worst 26x)**, so the naive form is not harmless from 2 km up on the tier-B adapter. 20 km and 20,000 km pass. CON-11 forbids shader values of planet-radius magnitude, and ADR 0035 keeps CON-11 in full for presentation data.

## Decision (proposed)
1. **Model:** Hillaire 2020 with Earth defaults; its parameters (Rayleigh and Mie scattering, ozone, scale heights, top of atmosphere H = 100 km) are look data per body (ADR 0042 point 6), not world data.
2. **LUTs:** transmittance 256x64 and multiple scattering 32x32 are computed **on the CPU in f64 at body load** and uploaded, not cached (they stay presentation data). The sky-view LUT (192x108) and the aerial-perspective volume (32x32x32) are computed **on the GPU per frame** for cameras inside the atmosphere; from outside it (orbit) the sky composite raymarches per pixel from the atmosphere's entry point, which is what the 20,000 km tests cover.
3. **CPU reference** (GPU-free module `frame::look::atmosphere`, CON-02): one f64 implementation that produces the static LUTs and serves as the oracle. The tier-B oracle uses the **same** texel-to-direction mapping, sample count and placement, the same static LUTs with an f64 bilinear lookup, at the f32-rounded inputs, so the bound measures arithmetic, not discretisation; a separate tier-A test bounds the discretisation against the converged integral. Static LUTs are sampled with `textureLoad` and a manual lerp (no 8-bit hardware filter weights).
4. **Class and bounds** (ADR 0035): presentation data; radiance in units of top-of-atmosphere solar illuminance = 1 (daylight sky about 0.05). Bound: per-frame LUT texels within 1 % relative plus an absolute 1e-4 (dominant at twilight, intentionally), at camera altitudes 1 m, 2 km and 20 km with three sun angles including sunset; the per-pixel orbit path at 20,000 km with the same bound.
5. **CON-11 (v1.4, owner, 2026-10-15, ADR 0043):** the atmosphere is presentation shading and may use the planet radius and r directly when its declared bound holds, including temporal stability under camera motion at 1 m altitude. The known risk is not R's own rounding but cancellation between planet-sized values (r - R, r^2 - R^2: about 33 % wrong at 1 m altitude in naive f32, harmless from 2 km up). **Decided (SPIKE-09): the altitude-relative primitives are used at every altitude**, in one module (0 failures on the RTX over Vulkan and DX12 and on WARP, static and in the 1 m walk): the CPU passes h, c = h(2R + h) and r computed in f64; ground distance (mu < 0, D >= 0) `t = c / (-r mu + sqrt(D))` with `D = (r mu)^2 - c`; exit through the top of the atmosphere from inside with `c_top = (h - H)(2R + h + H)`: `t = -c_top / (r mu + sqrt(D_top))` for mu >= 0 and `t = -r mu + sqrt(D_top)` for mu < 0; sample altitude `h_s = (c + s(s + 2 r mu)) / (r_s + R)`; rho = sqrt(c) for the LUT mappings and the horizon angle (expected error by hand: about 1e-6 relative away from the horizon, up to 5e-4 of t at tangency, about 1 m at the top of the atmosphere).
6. **SPIKE-09 (done 2026-10-16, #176):** the primitives pass everywhere; the worst case is the planet limb at mu +-1e-6 seen from 20,000 km (0.83 of the bound on the RTX), where `(r mu)^2 - c` cancels in f32 in both forms. If the real passes drift there, the limb-stable discriminant `r^2 (mu - mu_h)(mu + mu_h)` with `mu_h` from f64 is the next step. Not covered by the spike: llvmpipe (run by CI's lavapipe job in tier B) and the real sky-view and aerial-perspective passes (their tier-B oracle covers them). The spike's plan was: the naive f32 form and the primitives against f64 at h = 1 m, 2 km, 20 km and 20,000 km, mu at -1, at the horizon +-1e-6 and +1, on WARP, llvmpipe and the RTX, plus a slow camera walk at 1 m altitude (the temporal-stability part of the bound: no stepping of sky-view or aerial-perspective values beyond the bound between frames). The simplest form that passes is used.
7. **Time box and fallback:** four days for the atmosphere passes; fallback if the GPU part misses 1 ms in the architect's perf window (the coder asks for it): Bruneton 2008 precomputed tables, which need the same primitives (point 5) and their own oracle, reported.
8. **Emissive light (owner's constraint 4):** emission from liquids (ADR 0041) is added to HDR colour and not scattered by the atmosphere in M5; the seam is a light list in the composite pass, deferred and recorded here.
9. **Debug views:** the four LUTs, transmittance along the view ray, the aerial-perspective slice at the camera's depth.

## Consequences
- Static LUTs cost nothing per frame and come with their oracle; per-frame cost (sky-view LUT, aerial-perspective volume, composite) is budgeted at 1 ms.
- Point 5 needs no further constitution decision (v1.4); SPIKE-09 picks the simplest passing form.
- Tests: tier A reference integrals against analytic cases and the primitives; tier B GPU LUTs against the reference; tier C goldens of ground, limb and sunset with fixed exposure.
