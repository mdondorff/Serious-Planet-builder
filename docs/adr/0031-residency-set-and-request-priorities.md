# 0031 Residency set and request priorities
Status: proposed (sketch by opus-planner, 2026-10-13, from the owner's decisions of 2026-10-13; completed by sonnet-explorer, 2026-10-15, with SPIKE-05's counts, M4 item 4)
Date: 2026-10-13
Source: owner's decisions 2026-10-13 (`docs/status.md`, M4 queue (c)); SPIKE-05 (`docs/spikes.md`); architecture report §4 and the M4 row; STRM-002, STRM-005, STRM-006, STRM-007, LOD-007, LOD-008; constitution CON-03, CON-04, CON-20, CON-22; ADRs 0032, 0033

## Context
Today the streamer wants exactly the nodes the frame draws, and that selection is cut to the view frustum (LOD-007). Looking around is therefore a cache miss: rotating the camera requests tiles that the position alone already determined. The owner's requirement: rotation (mouse look) must be close to free and must never fetch tiles that were not wanted before; a teleport must show something at once and then fill in everything the new position needs, not only the current view; prediction from the camera's velocity cannot be relied on (a teleport has none, a mouse is erratic).

What SPIKE-05 measured (cells 32, finest level 26, today's 44-byte vertex, 53.7 KB per tile; the eroded field):
- **The surroundings are cheap.** The position-based set (distance LOD, horizon cut, no frustum cut) against today's draw set: 1.0x at orbit-20000km, 1.6x at high-2000km, up to 3.0x at aerial-200km (1,969 against 655 nodes) and aerial-20km (3,192 against 1,106; 172 MB), 1.8x at low-2km, **1.23x at ground-1m (10,751 against 8,763 nodes; 578 MB against 471 MB)**. The by-hand 3-6x of the sketch was too high. With the finest level capped at 22 the ground-1m surroundings are 463 MB.
- **Today's frustum cut removes almost nothing near the ground**, because every node's sphere is padded by the global `max_height_m` (9 km or more) before the plane test: the draw set is already 81 % of the surroundings at ground-1m. A tile's own bounding sphere (exact radius of its vertices) would drop 67 % of the ground-1m tiles (2,913 of 8,757 kept) and loses no tile with a visible triangle in any standard view; 96 % of the ground-1m triangles are hidden.
- **Compact tiles** (SPIKE-06, 4.5 KB per tile): the ground-1m draw set is 39 MB. The surroundings scale to about 48 MB by the 1.23 ratio (scaled, not measured). Tile bytes are therefore not what limits the surroundings; ADR 0033 sizes the share.
- **Plan time and generation rate decide whether the scheme works in time** (ad hoc, not a perf window): the surroundings selection is 5.8 ms warm at ground-1m (0.03-2.9 ms warm elsewhere) but the eroded field's draw plan is 20 ms warm at ground-1m and 0.73-6.3 s warm (cold up to 6.5 s) at aerial-200km to orbit over the 16384 store (inferred, not profiled: mesh keys and tile origins evaluated every frame; item 7a confirms by ablation); generation is 1,217 eroded tiles/s on 20 threads with a lock-free source, 22 tiles/s through `StoreSource` as it is (one mutex per lattice read; five times slower than one thread). From empty, the ground-1m draw set takes about 7.2 s on 20 threads (surroundings 8.8 s) with a lock-free source, about 400 s as it is.
- Tile counts differ slightly by source: 8,763 is the tabled 16384-store draw set, 8,757 the 1024-bake set of the hidden-triangle pass.
- Not counted: the union of resident tiles along a flight, and the ancestors of the leaves (at most one third more tiles by the quadtree's geometry).

## Options
1. **Frustum-driven residency with a margin** (today plus a padded frustum). Fewest tiles. Rotation beyond the margin still misses; the margin is a guess.
2. **Position-driven residency** (chosen below): the wanted set is the distance-based LOD selection around the camera in all directions, cut only by the horizon. The frustum cuts the draw set only. 1.23x the draw set at the worst view, at most 3x elsewhere (measured); rotation is free.
3. **Velocity-predicted residency**. Good on smooth paths only; wrong for teleports and mouse look. Kept only as an extra (class 3 below).

## Decision (proposed)
1. **Two sets.** The *residency set* R is a pure function of the camera position, the LOD parameters (LOD-008 distances and per-node height bounds) and the horizon cut; the view direction is not an input. It holds the selected leaves in all directions plus their ancestors. The *draw set* D is R's selection cut to the frustum (LOD-007), so a frame draws only resident tiles, plus the parent fallback of STRM-002. D is a subset of R by construction (today's padded cut keeps 81 % of R at ground-1m, still a subset); the tight cut (point 7) is needed so that D is *small*, not for the subset property. *R_full* is this pure position function; *R_effective* is R_full after the degradation of point 4 and is what the streamer keeps. Both are computed in GPU-free crates (CON-03). R uses the draw set's LOD thresholds (same `tau`): SPIKE-05 shows the surroundings cost 1.23x at ground-1m (the largest in bytes) and up to 3.0x at altitude where totals are small, so a coarser surroundings `tau` is not needed while the share fits (point 4 is the only place where surroundings get coarser).
2. **The view direction orders requests and never decides whether a tile is wanted or kept.** Request order, highest class first:
   1. **missing nodes of D**, ancestors before descendants (never blank, STRM-002; assumption, to be confirmed in the scheduler change: a tile is generated from the field and does not need its parent's data, so the order is about what the screen shows first); within the class by screen-space error times visibility (STRM-005);
   2. **the rest of R**, coarse before fine, nearer before farther; the view direction may order within this class (in view first) but not change its membership;
   3. **prefetch**: R at the predicted position along the camera's translation velocity (translation only; yaw and pitch rates are never extrapolated), a bounded look-ahead on the injected clock, only while classes 1 and 2 are empty, and only into free share: it never evicts anything in R or D.
3. **Cancellation** (STRM-005): a queued or in-flight request that is in no class any more is cancelled. **Leaving the frustum is not a reason**: a request is cancelled only when its tile left R and the prefetch set, so a rotation cancels nothing and requests nothing (while R fits the share). When the travel direction changes, prefetch requests are cancelled first. An in-flight job is cancelled at its next stage boundary; a tile that completes anyway goes to the persistent cache (nothing is wasted) and is not uploaded. A tile that left R stays resident until the share needs the space (point 4, STRM-007), which gives hysteresis at the set's boundary.
4. **When the share is too small for R** (ADR 0033's terrain share), degrade in this order and never touch the view unless point 4c: (a) evict prefetch tiles and tiles outside R; (b) **make the surroundings coarser**: tiles of R that are not in D are replaced by their ancestors (sibling groups merged, farthest and finest first), so every direction stays covered by some level and nothing goes blank; (c) last resort, an explicit exception to LOD-008's `tau_px` guarantee that must be declared in the LOD delta and reported with the numbers: only if D with its ancestors and the roots (STRM-007) alone exceed the share, which ADR 0033 sizes against (so this is a budget failure, reported with the numbers), D itself coarsens through a larger `tau`. This is the single case where a rotation can request tiles (the new D needs finer levels than the degraded surroundings hold); the lag is a count-based measure of ADR 0032. (a merge is skipped when any sibling or descendant is in D, so ancestors never overlap D tiles.) Pure function of (camera, parameters, share in slots), so tier A tests it with a deliberately small share.
5. **Teleport** is a camera move with no velocity: class 1 fills first (coarse at once from the resident roots, sharpening level by level; the frame after the move may draw from a stale R, which STRM-002 covers through the resident roots), class 2 follows, prefetch restarts from the new position. How long that takes is bounded by the plan cost and the generation rate quoted above (points 8a, 8b); its detail-lag budget is defined in ADR 0032. A full ground-1m fill takes about 7.2 s (surroundings 8.8 s) on 20 threads with a lock-free source and about 400 s today, so ADR 0032's "back under 1 level within 2 s" can only mean detail lag (coarse first), not the fill, and is unattainable before 7a and 7b.
6. All of it runs on the injected clock, I/O and spawner (CON-04), so the scheduler is tested at tier A with fakes. R is recomputed when the camera position changes and the plan and selection run off the frame's critical path (frame pipelining, queue item 7); the draw plan uses the newest finished R.
7. **The draw set's frustum cut uses per-tile height bounds** (LOD-008) instead of the global `max_height_m` pad (queue item 7c). SPIKE-05's exact-radius test is the upper bound of what it can remove (67 % of ground-1m tiles); the conservative bound will remove less. Acceptance in the implementation change: no tile with a visible triangle is cut on the ten standard views, with the spike's CPU depth pass as the oracle.
8. **Prerequisites, in the queue's order** (they change what the scheme costs, not what it decides): (a) plan cost (item 7a): a tile's origin height and mesh key computed once per tile, so a warm plan of unchanged tiles evaluates the field zero times and costs at most 8 ms at every standard view; (b) `StoreSource` concurrency (item 7b): thread-count-independent tiles (CON-14) and at least 80 % of the in-memory rate on 20 threads; (c) the tight cut of point 7. Until (a) and (b) are done, a teleport frame misses its budget and the fill takes minutes; the scheduler is not measured before then.

## Consequences
- Rotation needs no generation and no upload once R_full is resident and fits the share: testable at tier A (a 360 degree yaw at a fixed position requests nothing and cancels nothing). At the starting shares (2 GB and 3 GB) the worst standard view's R (578 MB at today's vertex, about 48 MB scaled to SPIKE-06's format) fits before ancestors and the flight union are added, so point 4 is a safety net and not the normal path.
- VRAM and generation work grow with the surroundings, by 1.23x at ground-1m and up to 3x at altitude (R is 2.8 to 578 MB over the standard views at today's vertex). ADR 0033 sets the share; this ADR only states the order of loss.
- A flight keeps tiles of earlier frames until the share needs them; that union is not counted yet and belongs in the residency manager's tests (STRM-007).
- Tests: tier A for the yaw case, the teleport fill order (ancestors before descendants), no cancellation on rotation, cancellation on leaving R and on a travel change, prefetch only into free share and never along a rotation, outside-in degradation under a small share (D untouched until point 4c), R independent of the view direction (a property test over random yaws), D a subset of R on the ten standard views; perf scenes per ADR 0032.
- The residency set and the draw set both enter the pinned per-view counts (CON-20, TEST-011): they are re-pinned as a declared change by items 7c and 7d.

## Spec deltas (applied by the implementation changes, queue item 7; no OpenSpec change is opened for the ADR)
- **STRM-005** (rewritten): requests are in three classes (missing D, rest of R, prefetch) ordered as in point 2; a request is cancelled only when its tile is in neither R nor the prefetch set, never for leaving the frustum; the view direction never changes R or the prefetch set. Scenarios: *Rotation requests nothing* (360 degree yaw at a fixed position, R fully resident and fitting the share; while R is still filling a rotation only reorders queued requests); *Small share* is a separate scenario, *Cancel* (a node leaves R before its job starts), *Prefetch is translation only* (yaw rate does not move the predicted position), *Order* (ancestors before descendants, D before the rest of R, prefetch last).
- **STRM-006** (ADR 0033 owns the share cap; this ADR owns only the order of loss): when demand exceeds the cap the order of loss is point 4 (outside R and prefetch first, then coarser surroundings, then D); scenario: *Small share* (D and roots stay at full detail while the surroundings coarsen, and the allocated total stays at or below the cap).
- **STRM-007** (reworded): the tiles never evicted are the roots and the current draw set with its ancestors (not "tiles used in the current frame"); eviction takes prefetch and tiles outside R before LRU order among the rest; capacity is counted in pooled slots (ADR 0033).
- **LOD-007**: the spec text already says "padded by the terrain height"; the code pads by the global `max_height_m`, so this is a code fix plus a scenario, not a rewrite: pad by the node's own height bound (LOD-008); D is a subset of R; scenario on the oracle of point 7.
- **New requirement (streaming-cache, STRM-012)**: the residency set is a pure function of the camera position, the LOD parameters and the share; yaw and pitch do not change it; R_full contains the draw set and the ancestors of its leaves; R_effective after degradation contains the draw set unless point 4c applies.

## Open questions for the owner
1. **The one exception to "rotation never fetches"** (point 4c and the last sentence of point 4): with a share too small for the surroundings, a turn can need tiles the degraded surroundings do not hold. Accept that as the price of the share, or require that the share always fit R at full detail (then a smaller share is a configuration error that refuses to start)? Recommended: accept; by the standard-view counts it does not occur at either tier (ancestors, at most one third more, and the flight union are not yet included).
2. **Prefetch look-ahead** (point 2.3): how far ahead along the travel (seconds of travel, in units of tiles of the target level)? Recommended: decide from the first teleport and dive measurements (ADR 0032); start at a few seconds at the current speed, capped by free share.
3. **Occlusion culling** would remove most of the rest of D (87 % of the ground-1m tiles have no visible triangle) but conflicts with CON-03; unchanged, the owner's decision, not part of this ADR.
