# 0053 The water pass: implementation readings of ADR 0041 points 4 to 9
Status: accepted (opus-planner, delegation, 2026-10-17; points 4 and 5 amended by 0056 (proposed); point 2 with the lit measure reported only and `shore_filter` default 1, the owner judges the shore in motion at the M5 gate)
Date: 2026-10-17
Source: ADR 0041 points 4 to 9, ADR 0051 points 6 and 8, ADR 0035, ADR 0043, ADR 0048, ADR 0042 point 2; the coastal flights and the measurements of the change; the planner's steer of 2026-10-17 (no new per-format storage)

## Context
ADR 0041 fixes the water pass's design: the terrain's grid at the water level, Gerstner waves with CPU-reduced phases, the shoreline from `W - h_morph`, a flicker metric. Building it found three places where the text does not hold as written (a per-tile tangent projection of the wave vector opens a seam, the metric's wording needs a definition a test can run, a culled seabed takes its water with it) and left two decisions open (ADR 0051 points 6 and 8).

## Decision
1. **The wave vectors are planet-fixed and each wave is weighted by its tangent share at the point** (reading of ADR 0041 point 5). Projecting `k` onto each tile's own tangent plane gives two tiles that hold one point two phase functions (a difference `(k_t1 - k_t2) . local` of about half a radian at a 10 km tile), a seam at every tile border and face edge. Instead every wave is a plane wave of the planet-fixed 3D frame, the CPU reduces `(k . O + phi - omega t) mod 2 pi` per tile origin in f64 and the shader adds `k . local`; a wave's weight is the product of its tangent share (a smoothstep from 0.25 to 0.6 of the length of its direction's part along the surface at the point), its resolution (a smoothstep from four to eight vertices per wavelength at the vertex's own spacing: the node's sample spacing times one plus the vertex's morph factor, so it is continuous across LOD borders and, unlike a fade by camera distance, right over deep water where the grid is spaced for the seabed's distance) and the shore (the amplitude falls to zero with the depth, from four times the sum of the amplitudes, so the vertical displacement is at most 0.57 of the depth). Each factor is continuous in the point, so no tile border can show. The wave directions are the vertices of a regular tetrahedron, so at every place at least two lie well along the surface. The largest `|k . local|` of a wave with weight over the scripted views is 17 rad, and it is bounded by construction (a wave with weight spans at most `0.82 cells / 4` wavelengths of a tile: about 1.3 cells, limit 1,000). Departure from ADR 0041 point 5 noted: the fade follows the vertex spacing (continuous in the morph factor), not the camera distance.
2. **The flicker metric** (reading of ADR 0041 point 6): the share of water pixels that change coverage between consecutive frames of a coastal flight after reprojection with the f64 poses. Each water pixel's ray meets the sea sphere at a point; the point is projected into the other frame and counts as changed when that frame shows no water in the 3 x 3 pixels around the position; a dry pixel is a hole when the other frame shows water all around its point and no terrain nearer the camera hides it; both directions are summed; points within four pixels' angle of the horizon, points that left the frame and points hidden by terrain nearer the second camera are not counted. It is measured on a calm sea (wave height 0), because with waves a grazing pixel sees a displaced surface that moves with the clock (intended motion, judged by the bounds of point 1). The class bands are discrete functions of the depth; a band thinner than a pixel (the film and foam at a grazing angle) comes and goes by sampling, so the exact-class count is reported and not budgeted. The lit shore is measured separately (`lit_shore_flash`, reported, not budgeted): over three consecutive frames, the share of shore-band pixels (film, foam, shallow) whose lit luminance leaves the mean of the other two, after reprojection, by more than 25 % (0.3 stop) of the largest; on the same two flights with the water's own contribution (lit over terrain alone, so the seabed's shading is out): glide 6.5 % without the `fwidth` filter and 16.2 % with it, descent 41.0 % without and 7.0 % with (WARP; the terrain alone flashes 6.3 % and 36.8 % at those pixels, a terrain-shading effect of the beach albedo bands that this change does not touch). The filter helps where the footprint is large and the steps small and hurts where the footprint changes quickly between frames; **decided by the planner (delegation, 2026-10-17): the lit measure and the exact-class share are reported, not budgeted; `shore_filter` defaults to 1 (what ADR 0041 point 6 asks for, and it fixes the low approach); the glide regression is real, so the owner judges the shore in motion at the gate (a look key, reversible; the two flights are on the contact sheet with the filter on and off).** The coverage budget is normative: 0.1 % of covered pixels; the first run measured 0.0000 % on a 6 m glide and on a descent from 300 m to 4 m over a beach of 1:50 (305,216 and 459,725 covered pixels).
3. **The frustum cut keeps water above a culled seabed.** The tight volume of a tile (its height bounds) decides the frustum cut; for a tile over deep water the volume sits at the seabed, so a camera low over the sea loses the water above seabed tiles the view does not see (a band of unwatered seabed along the bottom of the frame, found by the coastal flight). A field may name a water level (`HeightField::water_cull_level`, default none); `tile_volume` then also computes the sphere the frustum tests use, reaching up to the level. Distances, morph intervals and the bounds are unchanged (raising the bounds themselves empties the morph interval of fine tiles over deep water), so nothing moves for a field that names no level; the viewer's world names its sea level.
4. **The coast for a sea level other than 0** (ADR 0051 point 8): the field's coast stays the continentalness's. The pass draws water on every tile that holds sea wherever the level is above the terrain (`W - h_morph > 0`). A negative level moves the visible waterline seawards (the shelf above the level is dry although the field says water there), a positive level floods the land below it in tiles that hold sea and nowhere else; inland land below a raised level stays dry (no flood fill: it needs hydrology). A tile holds one level and one liquid, so the flooded extent follows the tile grid at coarse levels; the per-vertex mask (ADR 0051 point 6, option 3) removes that if it shows. Reversible; hydrology's producers replace it.
5. **Per-tile storage of W** (ADR 0051 point 6): option 1, the per-tile CPU query at plan time, no storage, no `MESH_VERSION` change, the compact slot pins unchanged. 25 queries per new tile, memoised; measured 3.6 us per new tile and 0.14 us per query on an in-memory bake of 256 and of 1024 cells (7,536 tiles of the scripted views), 0.01 us per tile warm. Not too slow, so options 2 and 3 are not taken. Limits: a dry basin below the level inside a tile that also holds sea is drawn flooded, a lake smaller than a coarse tile's sample spacing can be missed at that level, and a tile with two bodies shows the more frequent one.
6. **Budget** (ADR 0041 point 8, owner 2026-10-15): the water pass is the copy of the scene target and the draw of the water nodes; its GPU time is recorded per frame (`FrameTiming::water_gpu_ms`, the copy outside it) for the perf window of item 8; the numbers of this change are ad hoc.
7. **Look data.** The water presentation parameters (absorption scale, Fresnel F0, glint roughness, shore fade, foam width and strength, crest displacement, gravity, wave scale) are the look file's `[body.<n>.water]` table, schema version 4 (ADR 0042 point 6, ADR 0049 point 4); what a liquid is (colour, emission, viscosity, wave height and length) stays the definition's liquid table (ADR 0051 point 1).

## Known limits (reported by the numerics review, not fixed here)
- The flicker metric (point 2) cannot see a waterline that crawls by one pixel in a pattern (it counts isolated pixels, with a 3 x 3 tolerance); the measured 0.0000 % is evidence against pixel noise, not against crawl. A line-crawl count is a later refinement.
- ADR 0041 point 6's `fwidth` filter is implemented (look key `water.shore_filter`, default 1: the shore cover, the foam's rise and its edges are stretched by that multiple of the pixel's change of depth; the class view stays discrete) with a mixed effect on the lit measure (point 2), which the owner judges at the gate.
- `choppy` is a multiple of the amplitude, the shading normal ignores it, and a liquid of short high waves with a large `choppy` can fold the surface (the sum of `choppy x k x amplitude` above 1); the Earth row is far below.
- The water draws the tile's skirts: where a water tile borders a tile without water the skirt hangs a short curtain of water at the tile border (the tile grid's limit of point 5).
- `WaterTileCache` is keyed by tile only and never shrinks within a run; a change of field or sea level needs `clear`.

## Consequences
- ADR 0041 stands; this ADR is the normative home of what its text leaves to the code.
- The water pass reads W only through the plan: hydrology's producers plug into `WaterSource` and the pass does not change.
- The frame-target share carries one more HDR-sized target (the scene copy), 103,926,032 B for the atmosphere frame with water at 1440p of 181,010,432 B.
