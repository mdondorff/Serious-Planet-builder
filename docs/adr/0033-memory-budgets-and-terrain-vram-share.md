# 0033 Memory budgets and terrain's VRAM share
Status: proposed (skeleton by opus-planner, 2026-10-13; the principle is the owner's decision of 2026-10-13; completed by sonnet-explorer, 2026-10-15, from SPIKE-05, SPIKE-06 and SPIKE-07, M4 item 5)
Date: 2026-10-13
Source: owner's decisions 2026-10-13 (`docs/status.md`, M4 queue (b) and (d)) and 2026-10-14 (pooled slots, review items B and E); SPIKE-05, SPIKE-06, SPIKE-07 (`docs/spikes.md`); `docs/background/optimization-brainstorm-review.md` items B and E; STRM-006, STRM-007; constitution CON-11, CON-13, CON-14, CON-20, CON-21; ADRs 0031, 0032

## Context
CON-21 caps total VRAM (8 GB mid tier, 14 GB high tier), RAM (8 GB) and GPU uploads (2 ms per frame); nothing reserves VRAM for anything but terrain. The owner wants room for other content later (ground cover, objects, a game). What the spikes measured (eroded field, cells 32, finest level 26, `docs/spikes.md`; counts, not perf-window timings):
- Today's tile is 1,221 vertices x 44 B = **53.7 KB**. The ground-1m draw set is 8,763 tiles, **471 MB**; the position-based residency set R (ADR 0031) is 10,751 tiles, **578 MB**, 1.23x the draw set (up to 3.0x at altitude, where totals are small; 2.8 to 578 MB over the ten standard views). The earlier hand estimates ("3-6x", "8,735 nodes", "0.4 GB") are replaced by these. Not counted: ancestors (at most one third more) and the union along a flight.
- SPIKE-06's compact tile (a texel per grid vertex in a pooled atlas, a 128 B record per tile) is **4.5 KB** with snorm8 normals (12x smaller; R at ground-1m 48 MB) and **8.8 KB** with 16-bit normals (see point 4). On the RTX, same process, it was 0.8-0.93x the GPU time of the vertex format with the exact morph target; absolute GPU times between runs are not comparable (see Consequences).
- SPIKE-07: capping the finest level at 22 (7.46 cm vertex, 2.4 m tile) removes 24 % of the ground-1m tiles (6,633 against 8,757) with no visible change (0.19 % of pixels differ by more than 8); height queries use `height(dir)`, never the mesh.
- Generation is 1,217 eroded tiles/s on 20 threads with a lock-free source; plan cost and `StoreSource` concurrency are ADR 0031's prerequisites. Nothing about RAM or upload time has been measured yet (points 6 and 7 say so).

## Decision (principle decided by the owner 2026-10-13; the rest proposed)
1. **Terrain has its own VRAM share**, a count-based cap on allocated terrain bytes (tile pools, slot records, vertex pool for levels 0-2, index buffers, the per-node records), asserted in tests on any adapter (CON-20) and separate from the tier cap: **2 GB at the mid tier (of 8 GB), 3 GB at the high tier (of 14 GB)**, as the owner set them. The measurements say terrain will not come near them: R with ancestors at the worst standard view is about 14,300 slots, **6 % of the mid share** (about 127 MB at 8.8 KB per slot), 4.2 % of the high share. The share is therefore a ceiling that also holds the flight's history, not a target; terrain could fall to 0.5 GB and still hold four times the worst R. Recommended: keep 2 and 3 GB (it is the owner's choice what the remainder buys). The rest of the cap (6 GB and 11 GB) is for everything else, including depth and colour targets, and terrain never uses it. Unit of the cap: **slots** (point 3), so the test needs no device.
2. **Pin the headroom as a test**: on each standard view, slots(R with ancestors) <= 10 % of the mid-tier share and the ground-1m draw set plus ancestors <= 5 %. A change that raises the demand beyond that fails with the numbers; ADR 0031 point 4 (degradation) stays the safety net and is tested with a deliberately small share.
3. **Pooled fixed-size slots.** Every tile lives in a slot of a pool with a free list, replacing one buffer per tile: exact accounting against the share, no allocator churn, the precondition for indirect multi-draw (review items B, C). A slot is `(cells + 1)^2` texels: **33 x 33 at cells 32, 65 x 65 at cells 64**. Texture pools are 4096 x 4096 atlases (134 MB at 8 B per texel; 124 x 124 = 15,376 slots at cells 32, 63 x 63 = 3,969 at cells 64), allocated on demand up to the share: **16 atlases = 246,016 slots at the mid share, 22 atlases = 338,272 at the high share**. Atlas size, atlas count and how a draw names its slot (a texture index per draw, a texture array, a binding array: the choice must work on WARP and on the RTX, and is made in the implementation change by a tier-C differential) are open decision points; the slot size and the accounting are not. A freed slot is reused only after the frames that may still read it have retired (frame pipelining; counted in frames). Levels 0-2 (at most 126 tiles, 6.8 MB at 54 KB) keep the 44-byte vertex format in a small vertex pool of the same discipline.
4. **Tile format: SPIKE-06's compact format for levels 3 and finer**, with the **exact** morph target (neighbour heights read from the atlas; the approximate variant moves the coarse position by metres at level 6-8 and fails the steep-field test, so it is out unless a perf window at equal clock state shows the GPU limiting). Texel = 16-bit tile-relative height and **two 16-bit octahedral normal components (8 B per texel, 8,712 B per tile plus the 128 B record = 8.8 KB at cells 32; 33.9 KB at cells 64)**. Why 16 bit: CON-14 gives normals 0.01 degrees; 16-bit octahedral components are about 0.002 degrees by calculation (to be confirmed by a tier-B test against the CPU normal), so the format meets CON-14 under any reading. The cost against snorm8 (0.9 degrees, 4.5 KB) is 4.3 KB per tile (95 MB instead of 48 MB for R at ground-1m), which the share absorbs 20 times over. The owner's optional reading (a stored normal is a render attribute made from the canonical CPU normal, an encoding and not a second implementation) would let snorm8 return; it is open question 2, not needed. The format bumps MESH_VERSION (or a tile-format version) and changes every golden, through `golden-review`.
5. **CON-13 guard.** Height quantisation is range / 65,535 of the tile, so 1 mm at the finest level holds while a finest tile has at most about **65 m of relief**. The tile builder knows the range: a tile at the finest level (and any tile whose step would exceed 1 mm where CON-13 applies) with more than 65 m of relief **falls back to the vertex format** (the pool of point 3, which then has to hold more than 126 tiles; its size is a count in the share) or to a wider height encoding (32-bit, 12 B per texel; the owner may prefer it). At cap 22 the edge is 2.4 m, so the guard needs a cliff steeper than 87 degrees; tier A pins zero fallbacks on the standard views and a tier-B case on the steep stress field exercises one.
6. **The finest level: cap at 22 for cells 32 (21 for cells 64).** 6,633 draw tiles and 8,588 in R at ground-1m against 8,757 and 10,712 at 26; with the compact format the bytes saved are small (58.7 against 77.5 MB at 8.8 KB), the cap pays in draw calls (-19 % at 1 m) and generation time. Level 23 (3.7 cm, -18 %, mesh within 0.03 mm of the field) is the cautious choice; at 22 the mesh is up to 1.8 mm off the field on a steep tile (irrelevant for collision, which uses `height(dir)`). Keep 26 only if a later field gains content below about 12 cm. The cap is a LOD parameter, so it is reversible.
7. **Detail below the finest level is visual only** (normals and shading; no height, no change to `height(dir)`, collision, picking, cache keys or the mesh): a GPU function of tile-local quantities with no CON-07 field implementation. If it ever has to change heights it becomes a field layer with a CPU reference (not recommended now). **Whether to build it at all is the owner's call on the frames** (`docs/spikes/spike-07-detail.jpg`): at the field's amplitude it is a faint mottling, it needs a gain above 1 (an invented design choice) to be seen. If built: CON-11 caveat, SPIKE-07's term sends absolute lattice cells (about 1e9) in the node uniform, used in exact wrapping integer arithmetic but not tile-local in the letter of CON-11; production sends the cell index **pre-multiplied by the hash constants as u32**, which gives the same hash with no lattice index in the shader, plus the 300-cells-per-node rule (worst 2.8e-3 degrees against CON-14's 0.01), a debug view (CON-18) and tier-C images.
8. **RAM** (CON-21: 8 GB for the process; terrain's proposed share **3 GB**). Components, with what is measured:
   - global bake blocks in memory: at most 20 blocks of 1024 cells (4 channels x 1025^2 x 4 B = 16.8 MB) = about **340 MB** (derived from the block counter during a bake, not a process measurement; the runtime reader's block cache size is not set yet);
   - tile pack index (ADR 0019: sorted `(sort key, key, offset, length)` entries, roughly 40 B per entry): **not measured**; proposal <= 1 GB, which the pack's size cap (ADR 0029) must keep entry counts under;
   - CPU copies of tiles: none kept once uploaded (the pack holds them); staged and in-flight tiles only, bounded by the per-frame upload cap (point 9) times the frames in flight plus one tile per generation thread (under 1 MB);
   - plan caches (`SelectionCache`, mesh keys) and wgpu/driver host memory: **not measured**.
   Proposal: bake blocks <= 0.5 GB, pack index <= 1 GB, staging <= 0.1 GB, plan caches <= 0.1 GB, the rest of the 3 GB as slack; the other 5 GB belong to the process outside terrain. Count-based tests (CON-20): blocks alive, index entries, staged bytes against their caps at tier A. The process working set is measured in the dive perf scene (ADR 0032) in a perf window, not in a test.
9. **Upload bytes per frame** (CON-21: <= 2 ms). The cap is a byte count, asserted at tier A, applied to tile data (slot texels and records): excess is deferred to the next frame in ADR 0031's class order. Starting value **1 MiB per frame (about 118 slots)**; the time it costs is **not measured** and is fixed in a perf window (the 2 ms is the pass criterion, the byte cap follows from it). For scale: sustained generation of 1,217 tiles/s is 10.8 MB/s, about 20 slots (177 KB) per 60 Hz frame, so the cap has five times headroom; a full ground-1m fill is 77 MB (8,763 slots) and takes 74 frames at the cap, short against the 7.2 s generation fill (ADR 0031 point 5). Separate and unresolved: the per-draw node records written every frame (368 B each in SPIKE-06's layout, 3.2 MB at ground-1m packed, 4.5 MB at the 512 B dynamic stride) are a CPU write into a ring, not a tile upload; whether they count against the 2 ms is decided by the perf window (open question 5).
10. **Cells 32 or 64** is decided with the slot size, not before the data: cells 64 gives 3.3x fewer nodes at equal spacing (CPU plan and encode) for 13 % more bytes (slot 33.9 KB) and 4x the generation time per tile (35 against 8.8 ms), so a teleport waits longer for its first fine tile. **Start at cells 32 with cap 22**; reopen after pooled slots and indirect multi-draw if the ground-1m CPU time is still over budget (the first M4 perf window). Cells is a parameter; changing it changes goldens and plan snapshots.
11. When demand exceeds the share, ADR 0031 point 4 decides what degrades, in slots.

## Consequences
- STRM-006 gains the terrain share and the upload cap as their own caps (spec delta below); tier-A tests at both tiers on a fake device budget.
- The format change is the largest single golden change of M4: MESH_VERSION bump, all goldens through `golden-review`, the level and relief views were within a fraction of a pixel in the spike. It is built after the pooled allocator and indirect multi-draw (queue item 7's order).
- **GPU clock (queue item 6b).** The compact format is adopted on bytes, slot accounting and CON-14, not on GPU time: SPIKE-06's GPU ratios are same-process ratios, and the same code read 2.85 to 6.0 ms between runs depending on the clock state. The A/B of the shipped shader, exact against approximate morph, atlas upload time, and the per-draw cost of a texture index must run in a perf window at P0 (or compare only at equal clock state), after the harness change of 6b. Until then no GPU-time budget in this ADR is claimed.
- Needs a perf window (not claimed here): upload time per frame at the 1 MiB cap and the 2 ms criterion; the node-record write cost; the process working set along the dive; the 16-bit normal decode cost in the vertex stage; the pack index's real bytes per entry.
- The terrain share leaves 6 GB (mid) and 11 GB (high) of the tier cap to everything else; later shares for ground cover and objects come from that remainder (a later ADR).
- A visual-only detail term costs no cache version, no spec change to heights and no collision change; a height-changing one would be a field layer.

## Spec deltas (applied by the implementation changes, queue item 7; no OpenSpec change is opened for the ADR)
- **STRM-006** (rewritten): *Terrain share*: allocated terrain bytes (pools, records, vertex pool, index buffers, node records) never exceed the tier's terrain share (2 GB mid, 3 GB high, configurable), counted in slots against the pool configuration, tested without a device; *Cap* (exists): when demand exceeds the share the allocated total stays at or below it, with ADR 0031's order of loss; *Upload cap*: bytes uploaded in a frame never exceed the configured cap and the excess is deferred in class order; *Slot accounting*: allocated plus free slots equal capacity, a slot is not reused while a frame in flight can read it; *Headroom*: slots(R with ancestors) at the standard views stay within the pinned fractions of the share (point 2); *RAM*: blocks alive, index entries and staged bytes stay within their caps.
- **STRM-007** (ADR 0031's rewording): capacity is counted in pooled slots.
- **New requirement (terrain-lod or the mesh spec; id assigned by the implementation change)**: the compact tile format (texel layout, 16-bit height and normal components, the record, exact morph target), the normal agreeing with the CPU normal within 0.01 degrees (tier B), levels 0-2 in the vertex format, CON-13's 1 mm at the finest level with the relief guard of point 5 (fallback counted), the finest-level cap as a LOD parameter (22 at cells 32), and no planet-radius value in the shader (REND-005's scan).
- **MESH_VERSION / pack payload**: a tile-format version and its payload codec for persisted tiles, decided in the implementation change.

## Open questions for the owner
1. **Shares.** Keep 2 GB / 3 GB although terrain needs about 6 % of the mid share? Recommended: keep; a lower share only helps if other content needs the room before it is built.
2. **Normal precision.** 16-bit components (8.8 KB per tile, CON-14 met under any reading, recommended) or snorm8 (4.5 KB, 0.9 degrees, needs your reading that a stored normal is an encoding)?
3. **Finest level.** Cap 22 (recommended), 23 (cautious) or keep 26? And the CON-13 guard's fallback: vertex format per tile (recommended) or a wider height encoding?
4. **The visual detail term below the finest level**: build it (gain, look) or not? It is optional and changes no geometry.
5. **Upload counting**: does the per-frame write of node records (3-4.5 MB at ground-1m) count against the 2 ms of CON-21, or only tile data? The perf window measures both.
6. **RAM split**: accept the proposal (3 GB terrain: 0.5 bake blocks, 1 pack index, 0.1 staging, 0.1 plan caches, slack) as a starting point until a perf window measures the working set?
7. **Cells 32 or 64**: start at 32 and decide after the first perf window with pooled slots and indirect draws, or decide now?
