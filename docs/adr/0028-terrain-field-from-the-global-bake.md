# 0028 The terrain field: a pure function of the direction over the global bake
Status: proposed (sonnet-explorer, 2026-10-12; answers the architect's four questions on the height_at plan; easy to reverse until a terrain field is stored; build on it while proposed, ship no stored terrain field before ADRs 0027 and 0028 are accepted)
Date: 2026-10-12
Source: ADR 0027 (reading the global bake, proposed), ADR 0026, ADR 0016 (height-aware LOD), ADR 0025 point 2 (what cache-verify compares), ADR 0022, ADR 0006; constitution CON-05, CON-08, CON-11, CON-14; COORD-007

## Context
The first regional field turns the baked global channels into terrain heights: elevation from the bake plus local detail noise. The height pipeline is direction-based (`HeightField::height(dir)`: tile samples, tile centres, plan origins, camera ground clearance, node selection bounds, cache-verify regeneration). ADR 0027 reads the bake at integer tile-sample positions, which a direction-based pipeline cannot supply without a second code path. The first plan (a `height_at(tile, k, l, dir)` method beside `height`) gives two paths that can disagree in the last bits for one world point; this ADR drops it.

## Decision
1. **One path: `height(dir)` is the field.** The terrain field is a pure function of the unit direction: `direction_to_face` gives the face and face coordinates, the bake's lattice position comes from them in floating point, the blend is the edge-symmetric blend of ADR 0027 point 3 (`generators::global::read_direction`), and the detail noise is a function of the direction. There is no second path (no `height_at`). Every consumer calls `height(dir)`: tile samples, tile centres and plan origins, camera ground clearance and collision, plan snapshots, mesh keys, and cache-verify regeneration (ADR 0025 point 2 stays as written: the regeneration is the field's height at the sample's direction; nothing in 0025 is amended). The integer-position reader of ADR 0027 (`read_lattice`, `read_tile_sample`) serves consumers that process whole tile arrays with halos; the terrain field does not use it.
2. **Parent, child and neighbour agreement is by direction bits.** A sample of a tile is the height at `TileId::sample_direction`, which is computed from the integer face lattice index (exact), and the same world point sampled by a parent tile, a child tile or a neighbour across a face edge or a cube corner has identical direction bits (COORD-007; tier-A tests of GEN-004 and GEN-013 count 12 (res - 1) + 8 distinct border directions at 256 cells). `height` is a pure function of those bits, so the heights are identical bits (same `f64`, same rounding to `f32`). The choice of face for a direction on a face edge or at a corner is a deterministic function of the direction (`direction_to_face` breaks ties in a fixed order), so a point on an edge reads the same cell and weights whichever tile asked. Continuity across an edge, for nearby points that are different directions, follows from the bake holding equal samples on both faces. The integer position is not what guarantees agreement here; the purity of `height` is.
3. **LOD bounds are conservative by a Lipschitz argument.** `height_bounds(centre, theta)` for the terrain field reads the face coordinates `(s, t)` of the centre and the lattice rectangle `centre +- L theta` with `L = (4 / pi) sqrt(3/2)` face coordinates per radian (the face coordinate is `(4 / pi) atan(u)`, and moving by an angle `theta` changes the angle `atan(u)` within the face's `(n, u)` plane by at most `theta / cos(beta)` where `beta` is the angle out of that plane, at most `asin(1 / sqrt 3)` inside a face). If the rectangle lies inside the face, every direction within `theta` of the centre is inside it: a geodesic from the centre that left the rectangle would first cross its boundary, still inside the face, after travelling more than `theta`, which the bound forbids. The bounds are the minimum and maximum elevation over the lattice cells the rectangle touches, widened by one cell (the blend reads the cell and its far neighbour) and by the maximum detail amplitude, and the blend is a convex combination, so it stays inside the hull of its samples. A rectangle that leaves the face, or touches more than 4096 cells, takes the global range `+-max_abs_height_m` (always sound). The margin is `L theta (1 + 1e-9)` plus one cell plus `1e-6` m for rounding in the blend; a tier-A test samples random caps and checks every height lies inside, and another that the cap rectangle contains the face coordinates of boundary directions. ADR 0016's height-aware selection depends only on soundness and on bounds hugging the terrain, both tested.
4. **Stencils across face edges.** A local filter that needs neighbours (a slope or an erosion stencil, task 5) is written as a function of the direction: it evaluates the base field at directions offset by angles (like `surface_normal_field`), each through `height`'s own face choice, so it crosses face edges without any halo and agrees with every tile that asks. The clamped halo of ADR 0027 point 4 therefore does not touch any field in M3; it only limits tile-array consumers, which wait for a reader that continues onto the neighbour face's blocks through the integer face-edge mapping (a new reader version) before they run on tiles at a face edge.
5. **Keys.** The terrain field's key chains the bake: the 64-bit `inputs_hash` of the field covers its read set and the bake's stage key, and the persistent height key carries the bake's stage key as its source (`HeightField::source_key`, STRM-003 `source`), so a bake change re-keys exactly the tiles and meshes made from it (ADR 0022: one upstream key). The implementation id is new (`cpu-terrain-v1`), so no existing golden, plan snapshot or known answer moves.
6. **Detail.** Local detail is `layer_noise` fBm in the direction, from a base frequency above the bake's resolution down to about 40 m, with an amplitude scaled by a ruggedness factor derived from the interpolated channels (rougher where the ground is high, smooth in the ocean), bounded by a stated maximum. The amplitude and frequency are chosen so that planning at the production parameters (cells 32, ratio 240) is not rejected for steepness (ADR 0016); the test plans the standard flight at those parameters.

## Consequences
- No new pipeline path; direction-based consumers stay consistent by construction.
- `HeightField` gains `source_key` (default `None`); `height_key` uses it.
- The field is slower per sample than the reference (a bake read and several detail octaves); the 3 detail octaves that matter at coarse LOD are the same function, nothing is cached per tile.
