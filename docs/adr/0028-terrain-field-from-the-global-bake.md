# 0028 The terrain field: a pure function of the direction over the global bake
Status: accepted (owner's delegation of 2026-10-12: the architect accepts ADRs that reviewers find sound and unambiguous; accepted by opus-planner after the numerics review and its fixes (Lipschitz constant, f32 coverage, detail bound); drafted by sonnet-explorer)
Date: 2026-10-12
Source: ADR 0027 (reading the global bake, proposed), ADR 0026, ADR 0016 (height-aware LOD), ADR 0025 point 2 (what cache-verify compares), ADR 0022, ADR 0006; constitution CON-05, CON-08, CON-11, CON-14; COORD-007

## Context
The first regional field turns the baked global channels into terrain heights: elevation from the bake plus local detail noise. The height pipeline is direction-based (`HeightField::height(dir)`: tile samples, tile centres, plan origins, camera ground clearance, node selection bounds, cache-verify regeneration). ADR 0027 reads the bake at integer tile-sample positions, which a direction-based pipeline cannot supply without a second code path. The first plan (a `height_at(tile, k, l, dir)` method beside `height`) gives two paths that can disagree in the last bits for one world point; this ADR drops it.

## Decision
1. **One path: `height(dir)` is the field.** The terrain field is a pure function of the unit direction: `direction_to_face` gives the face and face coordinates, the bake's lattice position comes from them in floating point, the blend is the bilinear blend of the lattice cell with floating-point weights (`generators::global::read_direction`; no `mul_add`, no fast-math), and the detail noise is a function of the direction. Edge symmetry of the blend (ADR 0027 point 3) is not needed here: exactly one face is evaluated per direction (point 2). There is no second path (no `height_at`). Every consumer calls `height(dir)`: tile samples, tile centres and plan origins, camera ground clearance and collision, plan snapshots, mesh keys, and cache-verify regeneration (ADR 0025 point 2 stays as written: the regeneration is the field's height at the sample's direction; nothing in 0025 is amended). The integer-position reader of ADR 0027 (`read_lattice`, `read_tile_sample`) serves consumers that process whole tile arrays with halos; the terrain field does not use it.
2. **Parent, child and neighbour agreement is by direction bits.** A sample of a tile is the height at `TileId::sample_direction`, which is computed from the integer face lattice index (exact), and the same world point sampled by a parent tile, a child tile or a neighbour across a face edge or a cube corner has identical direction bits (COORD-007; tier-A tests of GEN-004 and GEN-013 count 12 (res - 1) + 8 distinct border directions at 256 cells). `height` is a pure function of those bits, so the heights are identical bits (same `f64`, same rounding to `f32`). The choice of face for a direction on a face edge or at a corner is a deterministic function of the direction (`direction_to_face` breaks ties in a fixed order), so a point on an edge reads the same cell and weights whichever tile asked. Continuity across an edge, for nearby points that are different directions, follows from the bake holding equal samples on both faces. The integer position is not what guarantees agreement here; the purity of `height` is.
3. **LOD bounds are conservative by a Lipschitz argument.** The face coordinate is `s = (4 / pi) atan(x / z)`; its gradient on the unit sphere has magnitude `(4 / pi) / sqrt(1 - y^2)`, where `y` is the component along the other face axis. Inside a face `y^2` is largest at the middle of an edge (`1/2`), so `L = (4 / pi) sqrt 2 = 1.80` there; the bounds use the extended coordinates up to `|u| <= 1.2` (face coordinate 1.14), where `y^2 <= 1.44 / 2.44` and the gradient is at most 1.99, and take `L = 2`. For a cap of radius `theta` with `L theta <= 0.1`, a geodesic of length at most `theta` from any cap direction in the cone of a face `F` stays within the extended region of `F`, so every such direction has face-`F` coordinates within `L theta` of the centre's: the faces to scan are those where the centre's extended coordinates are within `1 + L theta`, and in each the rectangle `centre +- L theta`, clipped to the face, holds every cap direction of that face (the extended coordinate is computed unclamped, `(4 / pi) atan(u)`). In each face the bounds are the blend at the anchor (the centre clamped to the face) plus or minus the largest difference of adjacent samples along each axis times the rectangle's half-width in lattice units (the blend is bilinear), intersected with the hull of the cells the rectangle touches (widened by one cell for the blend's far neighbour); the union over faces is widened by the detail (point 6). A cap with `L theta > 0.1`, or touching more than 4096 cells, takes the global range `+-max_abs_height_m` (always sound). The margins are `L theta (1 + 1e-9)` in the reach and, on the final bounds, `1e-6 m` plus `1.2e-7` of their magnitude (twice the half-ulp of `f32`), so the bounds hold the heights rounded to `f32` as meshes store them. Tier-A tests check every height of dense caps lies inside, with caps centred near edge midpoints (`s = 0`, `t` in `[0.8, 1]`, `theta` in `{0.02, 0.05, 0.1}`) and corners as well as random ones, that `f32`-rounded heights are covered, and that small caps hug the terrain. ADR 0016's height-aware selection depends only on soundness and on bounds hugging the terrain, both tested.
4. **Stencils across face edges.** A local filter that needs neighbours (a slope or an erosion stencil, task 5) is written as a function of the direction: it evaluates the base field at directions offset by angles (like `surface_normal_field`), each through `height`'s own face choice, so it crosses face edges without any halo and agrees with every tile that asks. The tangent frame that `surface_normal_field` builds switches axis on the planes `|x| = |y|`, a jump of the frame's orientation of order epsilon; that does not matter for a normal, but an anisotropic stencil or an erosion filter must not depend on the frame's orientation. The clamped halo of ADR 0027 point 4 therefore does not touch any field in M3; it only limits tile-array consumers, which wait for a reader that continues onto the neighbour face's blocks through the integer face-edge mapping (a new reader version) before they run on tiles at a face edge.
5. **Keys.** The terrain field's key chains the bake: the 64-bit `inputs_hash` of the field covers its read set and the bake's stage key, and the persistent height key carries the bake's stage key as its source (`HeightField::source_key`, STRM-003 `source`), so a bake change re-keys exactly the tiles and meshes made from it (ADR 0022: one upstream key). The implementation id is new (`cpu-terrain-v1`), so no existing golden, plan snapshot or known answer moves.
6. **Detail.** Local detail is `layer_noise` fBm in the direction (14 octaves from a base frequency of 64 per radius, persistence 0.45, normalised by the sum of the octave amplitudes, so the noise is in `[-1, 1]`), times an amplitude that is a nondecreasing function of the base elevation: 10 m over the ocean, 30 m on lowland growing to 300 m at 5 km (`detail_amplitude_m`). The detail is therefore bounded by `|detail| <= sum of |octave amplitude| / norm * max amplitude = 300 m`, a number (`DETAIL_MAX_M`), and over a cap by the per-octave noise bounds times the amplitude range of the cap's base bounds. The amplitude and frequency are chosen so that planning at the production parameters (cells 32, ratio 240) is not rejected for steepness (ADR 0016); the test plans the standard flight at those parameters.

## Consequences
- No new pipeline path; direction-based consumers stay consistent by construction.
- `HeightField` gains `source_key` (default `None`); `height_key` uses it.
- The field is slower per sample than the reference (a bake read and several detail octaves); the 3 detail octaves that matter at coarse LOD are the same function, nothing is cached per tile.
- The steepness check at the production parameters (cells 32, ratio 240) was first run on a bake of 256 cells per face edge; a finer bake resolves steeper global relief, so M3 acceptance repeats it on a bake at the production resolution (16384 cells per face edge, or a region of it with the same lattice).
