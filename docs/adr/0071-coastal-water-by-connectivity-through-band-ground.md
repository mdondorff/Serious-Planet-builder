# 0071 Coastal water by connectivity through low ground, not by a reach
Status: accepted (opus-planner, 2026-10-20; drafted by sonnet-explorer, third draft after two rounds of spec-guardian and numerics reviews). Amends ADR 0056 points 1 to 3 (the reach, the probe grid) and the coarse-tile rule of ADR 0058 that follows them. Point 3 (the algorithm, its sizes and times) is provisional until SPIKE-21 closes.
Date: 2026-10-20
Source: SPIKE-17 bug 2 (`spikes/owner-captures-2026-10-20/results/results.md`); ADR 0056; ADR 0058; ADR 0051 option 3; ATMO-019; GEN-022, GEN-024

## Context
- The owner's captures show the sea ending at a straight edge on a plain 10.7 to 13.5 m high in the bake, eroded 1 to 10 m under the level: the edge is `COAST_REACH_M` (100 km). The plain is connected to the ocean through ground the band rule calls possibly-under-the-level for 120 to 132 km.
- The reach tests distance, not connection: it floods a band pit behind any ridge within 100 km and dries a connected plain beyond it. Raising it floods every basin within the new distance.
- `B(W)` (ATMO-019, about 64 m at level 0) is the highest bake elevation at which **flat** ground can be cut under the level `W`. Steep ground is cut deeper (up to 0.75 `A(E)`), so `B(W)` is not an upper bound on where the sea can lie.
- The ocean channel (GEN-022) labels vertices by connected component with a tiled union-find, bit-identical for every block size, thread count and order; its seeds are the ocean vertices.
- The coarse summary (ADR 0058, GEN-024) holds no sea level, so one summary serves every level; the water decision of a tile must keep that.

## Options
- A. The reach raised to about 300 km: floods every basin within it. Rejected.
- B. One bit per vertex, "connected to the ocean through vertices at most `B(W)`": the level enters the store key and the summary, so a level change is a new pass over 11.7 GB. Fallback if C fails SPIKE-21.
- C. **Per vertex the lowest conduction bound at which it is wet** (the minimax path elevation from the ocean): one store serves every level and every conduction rule.
- D. Flood on demand per tile with a cache: unbounded work on the plan or job thread. Rejected.

## Decision (C)
1. **The wet floor.** Every bake vertex has an integer code `q(e) = floor(2 e) + 24000`, clamped to 1 .. 0xFFFE (half-metre steps from -12,000 m to +20,766 m; below the range the code is 1, above it 0xFFFE, so the clamps keep the wet set a superset); an unreadable or NaN elevation has the code 0xFFFF (+infinity) and an ocean vertex 0 (-infinity). The **floor** of a vertex is the minimum, over 8-neighbour paths from an ocean vertex to it, of the largest code along the path (an ocean start counts as 0); a vertex no path reaches has 0xFFFF. All passes work on these integers, so ties, order and the in-memory and stored paths are exact. Adjacency is 8 (a path through a cell with two diagonal low corners exists on the continuum). Blocks share their border row and column of vertices, so every 8-neighbour edge lies in a block; the shared vertices and the cube-face edge and corner copies are one node with one canonical id (GEN-022), and the copies' elevations are asserted equal. Sea vertices that are not ocean conduct and are wet by the same rule.
2. **The bound.** `C(W)` is the flat-ground band elevation `B(W)` (ATMO-019), required non-decreasing in `W`. A vertex is **wet** when `floor <= qC(W) = floor(2 C(W)) + 24000` (the code of `C`); the stored floor is rounded down, so this is a superset of the true wet set, and one shared function makes the comparison for the point query and the summary. Steep ground that is under the level at a higher bake elevation does not conduct (the existing limit of ATMO-019): a plain behind a steep pass that the reach flooded within 100 km is dry now (named, accepted by opus-planner; the bound is one constant, no re-bake). Conduction is by elevation alone, so the path may cross any ground at most `C(W)` high, a high interior plateau included.
3. **Storage and algorithm (provisional, SPIKE-21).** `floor` is a `u16` per vertex, block kind 6 beside the ocean bits (LZ4), implementation id `cpu-wet-floor-v1`, keyed by the ocean key and the sample key only (neither `B` nor the erosion version belongs in it). Three passes over the stored sample blocks and ocean bits: (a) per block, a union-find in increasing code with one virtual ocean node per block (its ocean vertices at code 0), emitting an edge whenever two components that hold terminals (border vertices or the ocean node) merge: at most `4 (N - 1) + 1` edges per block (4,097 for N = 1025), about 125 MB for the 1,536 blocks of a 16384-cell bake; (b) one serial pass over all edges, the per-block ocean nodes being one node, giving the floor of every border vertex; (c) per block a bucketed priority flood seeded with the border floors and the ocean vertices. The floors are unique minimums, so the result is bit-equal for every block size, thread count and order and equals a serial priority-flood used as the test oracle. If SPIKE-21 shows the memory or the time out of reach, option B is the fallback.
4. **Consumers.** A tile with no sea of its own carries the sea when one of its 25 samples is band (`band_at`, unchanged) and a vertex that contributes weight to the sample (the interpolation stencil of GEN-023, `weighted_vertices`) is wet; `SeaProducer::water_at` takes `ocean || wet`. `COAST_REACH_M`, `REACH_PROBES` and the reach arithmetic go. The coarse summary stores per cell the lowest `floor` over the lattice corners of its cells (a `u16`, a pure function of the block); a tile at or above the block level is wet when a cell it touches has a wet floor and may hold band, a superset, and a parent is wet wherever its children are, by the union of the cells. Below the block level a fine tile can be wet where its parent is not, as with the reach today; the pass draws water only where the terrain is under the level, so a dry parent never hides water a wet child draws, and the coarse tiles above the block level keep the view complete (ADR 0058).
5. **The rest of ADR 0056 stands:** own-sea tiles, the per-vertex flood of the pass (`W - h_morph > 0`), the tile job, the book, the wave fade. `MESH_VERSION` and `GLOBAL_VERSION` are unchanged: the sample blocks and the ocean blocks do not change; the summary gets a new codec version. The flood, the store and the point query live in GPU-free crates (CON-03); no shader changes.

## Consequences
- An inland basin enclosed on every side by ground above `C(W)` stays dry (owner constraint 2). A basin joined to the ocean through ground at most `C(W)` over any distance is wet where its fine terrain is under the level, a Dead-Sea-like depression included; inside 100 km that was already so.
- Store: about +3.2 GB raw (2 bytes x 1.6e9 vertices), LZ4 less; one command writes it, a store without kind 6 is refused with that command (ADR 0058 point 5). Estimates (SPIKE-21 measures them): 3 to 5 minutes on 20 threads, 125 MB of merger.
- Goldens: `water_class_coast_*` and the lit coast pictures move (lit/water classes: the owner's `/bless`).
- Specs: ATMO-019 and ATMO-012 (selection), GEN-022 (the channel and the shared borders), GEN-024 (summary field and version), STRM-023/025 (kind 6, keys), the viewer's error for a missing kind. Tests: the three passes equal a serial priority flood over block sizes, thread counts and orders, on a bay behind a ridge beside a low border component, a path that leaves and re-enters a block, a diagonal-only link across a block corner and at a cube corner; code encode and decode at the limits (NaN, plus and minus infinity, -0.0, clamps); a rounding-down superset test; monotone in the level on a grid of `W`; a lagoon behind a low barrier is wet; the cliff coast keeps its water; an enclosed basin and a pit behind a ridge stay dry; the stored path equals the in-memory path; the SPIKE-17 test passes.
- Learning to record: `B(W)` bounds flat-ground incision only.
