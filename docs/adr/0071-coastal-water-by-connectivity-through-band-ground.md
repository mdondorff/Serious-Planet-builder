# 0071 Coastal water by connectivity through band ground, not by a reach
Status: proposed (sonnet-explorer, 2026-10-20, for opus-planner). Amends ADR 0056 points 1 to 3 (the reach, the probe grid) and the coarse-tile rule of ADR 0058 that follows them.
Date: 2026-10-20
Source: SPIKE-17 bug 2 (`spikes/owner-captures-2026-10-20/results/results.md`: frames 5161, 6193, 13883; the failing test `water_reach.rs`); ADR 0056; ADR 0058; ADR 0051 option 3; ATMO-019; GEN-022, GEN-024

## Context
- The owner's captures show the sea ending at a straight edge on a plain whose bake elevation is 10.7 to 13.5 m and whose eroded terrain lies 1 to 10 m under the level. The edge is `COAST_REACH_M`, 100 km from the nearest ocean vertex.
- The plain is connected to the ocean through band ground for 120 to 132 km; 59 to 61 % of the 29,000 to 30,500 connected band cells of the flood on a 600 km grid are farther than 100 km from every ocean vertex (up to 289 km).
- The reach tests distance, not connection: it floods a band pit behind any ridge within 100 km and dries a connected plain beyond it. Raising it to 300 km covers this plain and floods every basin within that distance of the sea.
- A band sample is land with a bake elevation at most `B(W)` (about 64 m at level 0): the highest elevation at which flat ground can still be cut below the level (ATMO-019). Ground above `B(W)` is above the level on flat ground whatever the erosion does.
- The ocean channel (GEN-022) already labels bake vertices by connected component with a tiled union-find that is bit-identical for every block size, thread count and block order.

## Decision
1. **Which ground conducts.** A bake vertex is band when it is land and its elevation is at most `B(W)` (the existing predicate). A band vertex is **band-wet** when it lies in the same connected component of the sea-or-band vertices as an ocean vertex (the ocean channel's adjacency, cube-face edge copies merged). Ground above `B(W)` does not conduct. The test is necessary for the sea to reach a point on flat ground, not sufficient: the wet set contains the points the sea can reach, and the pass still draws water only where the fine terrain is under the level (`W - h_morph > 0`), so the excess is drawn dry.
2. **Physics against the reach.** Within 100 km the flood is a subset of what the reach floods (a band pit behind ground above `B(W)` is no longer wet: it fixes the inland basin the reach gets wrong); beyond 100 km it is new. The error that remains is a fine-scale pit under the level inside band-connected ground that fine ridges cut off from the sea: drawn flooded, as the reach drew it. Limits: a ridge above `B(W)` narrower than a bake cell (about 1 km) between two band cells is not seen; steep ground (slope factor above the floor) is not covered (ATMO-019 as before). A stricter conduction predicate (the mean cut, about 17 m under the level on plains, ADR 0056 context) would dry the estuary pits in 30 to 64 m ground close to the coast that the reach floods today, so it is not chosen; the threshold is one function (`conducting_elevation_m`) if the owner wants it tightened.
3. **A second bit per vertex.** The ocean pass is followed by a pass over the stored sample blocks and ocean bits: per block, local components of sea-or-band vertices, flagged when they contain an ocean vertex; the flags merge by the same union-find; the output is one bit per band vertex (stored like the ocean bits: LZ4, 1 bit per vertex, kind 5), bit-identical for every block size, thread count and block order and equal to a serial flood reference. Its key holds the ocean key, `B(W)` bits and the erosion version, so a new sea level or erosion re-keys it.
4. **Consumers.** `band_water` asks "one of the tile's 25 samples is band-wet" (the weighted neighbourhood of the vertices, as `ocean_bit_at` reads it) instead of probing a 51 x 51 disc: `COAST_REACH_M`, `REACH_PROBES` and the reach arithmetic go, and a tile costs at most 25 neighbourhood reads instead of up to 2,500. The coarse summary (GEN-024) gets a flag per cell: the cell holds a band-wet vertex or may hold one (a land corner under `B(W)` in a cell whose neighbourhood has one); the coarse answer stays a superset of the point query's, a parent wet wherever its children are.
5. **The rest of ADR 0056 stands:** own-sea tiles, the tile job computing the water, the book, the per-vertex flood, the pixel-footprint wave fade, the beach views. Nothing is stored in a tile or its key (`MESH_VERSION` unchanged).

## Consequences
- Re-bake: the heights are not recomputed. The new pass reads the stored sample blocks (11.7 GB at 16384 cells) and the ocean bits; the ocean pass cost about 2 % of the bake's thread time inside the bake (356 s on 20 threads), the flood pass is of the same size plus the block reads, so an estimate of 1 to 2 minutes on 20 threads when the blocks are stored (6 minutes if the whole bake is regenerated). A measurement replaces the estimate in the implementing PR; a stored bake without kind-5 blocks is refused with the command that writes them.
- Goldens: the `water_class_coast_*` and lit coast pictures move where a plain beyond 100 km is in view (and may lose water behind high ground within 100 km); lit and water classes, so the owner's `/bless`.
- Specs: ATMO-019 (modified: the rule, the coarse rule, the limits), GEN-022 (the band-wet channel), STRM-023 or the store kinds (kind 5), the failing SPIKE-17 test enters as a test of ATMO-019. Tests: the flood equals a serial reference over block sizes, threads and orders; an inland basin and a pit behind a ridge above `B(W)` stay dry; the plain of the captures is wet at the three gap tiles; parent wet where children are, over real bakes.
- Easy to reverse: the conduction predicate; the reach could return as a fallback for a bake without kind 5 (not planned).
